    \begin{abstract}
        The Vector Nodal Meshless Method (VNMM) provides a robust node-based framework for approximating $H(\mathbf{curl})$ vector fields on unstructured point clouds. In early formulations, including the recently stabilized complete linear $\mathcal{P}^1$ space with scale-invariant stencil qualification, spatial integration of the weak form relied strictly on background Cartesian integration cells. While effective, such cell-based integration suffers from two fundamental limitations: (i) the support stencils switch dynamically inside cells across Gauss points, rendering the integrand piecewise discontinuous and susceptible to under-integration artifacts, and (ii) constructing background grids compromises the truly meshless nature of the method in arbitrary geometries. In this paper, we develop and validate a \textit{truly cell-free} formulation of the VNMM based on the Meshless Local Petrov--Galerkin (MLPG) paradigm. By defining regular, independent circular local subdomains $\Omega_s^I$ centered at each node, all volumetric integrals are decoupled from any global background grid. We prove that classical weight-function test spaces (MLPG1) identically collapse on linear vector polynomial spaces by Stokes' and Gauss' theorems, necessitating the adoption of the Local Galerkin formulation (MLPG6) where vector test functions coincide locally with the complete trial basis ($\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{N}$ in $\Omega_s^I$). This choice produces closed-form, exact analytical local stiffness and mass matrices, totally eliminating numerical quadrature errors and yielding strictly symmetric positive-semidefinite global operators. Boundary conditions on perfectly conducting (PEC) walls are enforced via direct tangential nodal collocation, bypassing singular boundary integrals. The proposed VNMM-MLPG is systematically verified across four rigorous stages: (1) machine-precision Galerkin patch test satisfaction ($\sim 10^{-15}$) across seven affine states on regular and 25\% perturbed point clouds; (2) optimal asymptotic convergence via the Method of Manufactured Solutions (MMS), achieving $\mathcal{O}(h^{2.06})$ in $L^2$ and $\mathcal{O}(h^{1.24})$ in $H(\mathbf{curl})$; (3) physical spectral validation on a 2D PEC resonant cavity, reproducing the first 10 transverse electric modes with an average wavenumber error of $4.13\%$ and achieving total spectral purification via volumetric div-curl penalty ($s_{\mathrm{div}} \ge 10$); and (4) computational benchmarking demonstrating a $3.59\times$ assembly speedup and $36\%$ fewer matrix non-zeros compared to cell-based Gauss quadrature. The proposed formulation establishes a mathematically consistent, cell-free vector meshless framework for computational electromagnetics.
    \end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Computational electromagnetics (CEM) has traditionally relied on the finite element method (FEM) equipped with mixed edge elements (Nédélec/Whitney forms) \cite{nedelec1980mixed, monk2003finite, jin2014finite} to resolve vector boundary-value and eigenvalue problems in $H(\mathbf{curl})$. Edge elements assign tangential field components to elemental edges, enforcing trace continuity while naturally accommodating jump discontinuities across dielectric interfaces and satisfying the exact sequence of the de Rham complex. Consequently, irrotational gradient fields reside in the exact discrete nullspace of the curl operator ($\mathrm{grad} \, W_h \subset \ker(\mathrm{curl}_h)$), preventing the emergence of non-physical spurious eigenmodes \cite{bossavit1998computational, boffi2006finite}. Nevertheless, edge elements are constrained by the necessity of generating and maintaining conforming simplex meshes (triangles in 2D, tetrahedra in 3D). Mesh generation remains the primary computational bottleneck in complex CAD geometries, multi-scale devices, moving boundary problems, and topology optimization routines.

To circumvent mesh generation bottlenecks, meshless and meshfree methods have garnered sustained interest across solid mechanics, fluid dynamics, and wave physics \cite{belytschko1994element, liu2009mesh, fasshauer2007meshfree, cheng2014meshless}. In computational electromagnetics, early meshless adaptations employed scalar formulations for magnetic vector potentials \cite{ho2001moving, parreira2006efficient, soares2011meshless} or scalar Helmholtz equations \cite{nicomedes2012meshless}. Extending meshless concepts to vector fields governed by Maxwell's curl-curl equations proved substantially more challenging. Nodal collocated schemes with vector degrees of freedom typically induce derivative aliasing and severe spurious mode contamination due to the lack of discrete de Rham complex exactness on arbitrary point clouds \cite{lima2017edge}.

The Vector Nodal Meshless Method (VNMM), pioneered by Ortiz et al.~\cite{ortiz2021nodal, ortiz2023tese} and Gonçalves et al.~\cite{goncalves2023vector, goncalves2025tese}, established an innovative node-based vector interpolation strategy. Unlike scalar meshless schemes, VNMM associates each node $P_I$ not only with a Cartesian position $\mathbf{x}_I$ but also with a unit director vector $\mathbf{t}_I$, assigning a scalar degree of freedom $e_I = \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}_I) \cdot \mathbf{t}_I$ representing the directional projection of the electric field. In a recent companion study submitted to this journal \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}, the mathematical foundations of the 2D VNMM were systematically examined on unconstrained irregular point clouds. That study resolved several fundamental questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Incomplete Basis Stagnation:} The classical incomplete solenoidal vector basis ($\mathcal{L}^1$, consisting of 3 terms per stencil) was shown to suffer from a persistent $\mathcal{O}(1)$ error plateau in curl approximation due to dilatational derivative aliasing on irregular clouds. This was completely cured by establishing the complete linear vector space $\mathcal{P}_1 \times \mathcal{P}_1$ (requiring a 6-node stencil, denoted $\mathcal{P}^1$), which restored full linear completeness and optimal $\mathcal{O}(h)$ curl convergence.
    \item \textbf{Geometric Singularity Mitigation:} A scale-invariant determinant qualification criterion on a dimensionless placement matrix $\widehat{\mathbf{A}}$, coupled with adaptive $k$-d tree searches, was developed to automatically reject collinear and nearly singular node configurations without empirical parameter tuning.
    \item \textbf{Variational Div-Curl Stabilization:} Because dynamic support stencils across point clouds lack global trace conformity, discrete gradients leak into the positive curl spectrum. A regularized Ritz--Galerkin formulation with a volumetric div-curl penalty ($s_{\mathrm{div}}$) was introduced, successfully shifting spurious modes to higher frequencies while preserving physical cavity resonances.
\end{enumerate}

Despite these substantial advances, the formulation reported in \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026} left open a fundamental deficiency: \textbf{the reliance on a Cartesian background integration grid}. As classified by Belytschko et al.~\cite{belytschko1994element} and Atluri and Shen \cite{atluri2002meshless}, methods like the Element-Free Galerkin (EFG) are not \textit{truly cell-free} because evaluation of the global weak form requires an auxiliary background mesh of quadrature cells covering the domain $\Omega$. In the VNMM context, this background scaffolding creates acute numerical challenges:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Intra-Cell Stencil Discontinuities:} In cell-based integration, the support stencil of 6 nodes is selected point-by-point at each Gauss integration point $P_g$. Because nearest-neighbor stencils switch abruptly within the cell interior, the resulting shape functions and their derivatives are piecewise discontinuous across intra-cell boundaries. As demonstrated in \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}, under-integrating these cells (e.g., using $2 \times 2$ Gauss points) produces artificial numerical softening that deceptively masks a 140-dimensional numerical rank deficiency in the discrete curl operator. Suppressing this artifact required a dense, resolution-matched quadrature ($3 \times 3$ Gauss points per cell, exceeding 3,600 quadrature evaluations for a 441-node grid).
    \item \textbf{Loss of True Meshless Autonomy:} In non-convex geometries, geometries with curved boundaries, or 3D domains, constructing a background cell grid—and trimming cut cells along complex boundaries—is computationally equivalent to generating a finite element mesh, effectively forfeiting the principal practical advantage of meshless methods.
\end{itemize}

Several strategies could, in principle, address these integration difficulties, but each introduces severe complications:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Conforming Background Tessellation (Delaunay/Voronoi, SCNI):} Stabilized Conforming Nodal Integration (SCNI) \cite{chen2001stabilized} evaluates strains on Voronoi polygons. However, constructing Voronoi diagrams on irregular point clouds is geometrically brittle, sensitive to boundary slivers, and computationally burdensome in 3D.
    \item \textbf{Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) with Tangential Jump Penalties:} One could view shifting stencils as non-conforming elements and introduce surface integrals penalizing tangential trace jumps $[[\mathbf{n} \times \mathbf{E}]]$. However, in an arbitrary point cloud, the boundaries of local support domains form a dense web of overlapping circular and lens-shaped intersections. Computing line integrals along millions of curved internal segments is practically intractable in 2D and virtually impossible in 3D.
    \item \textbf{Meshless Local Petrov--Galerkin (MLPG):} Originally conceived by Atluri and Zhu \cite{atluri1998new, atluri2002meshless}, MLPG circumvents global background cells entirely by establishing weak forms over independent local subdomains $\Omega_s^I$ (typically circular disks or spheres) centered at each node. Because every subdomain is geometrically simple and autonomous, all volumetric and boundary integrals are decoupled from neighboring topology.
\end{itemize}

While MLPG has been successfully applied to scalar mechanics and scalar wave propagation \cite{sladek2001local, atluri2002meshless, nicomedes2012meshless}, its extension to vector electromagnetic formulations in $H(\mathbf{curl})$ remains virtually unexplored. Applying MLPG to the Vector Nodal Meshless Method presents unique theoretical and algebraic hurdles:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How should vector test functions $\mathbf{W}_I$ be chosen on $\Omega_s^I$? In classical MLPG1, weight functions vanishing on the boundary ($w(r_s) = 0$) are used. However, we prove mathematically in this paper that for any linear vector basis ($\mathcal{P}^1$), classical weight test functions cause the bilinear forms to vanish identically by Stokes' and Gauss' theorems!
    \item How can global symmetry and positive-semidefiniteness be guaranteed? Standard Petrov--Galerkin test-trial pairings produce unsymmetric system matrices, which are catastrophic for electromagnetic eigenvalue problems as they induce complex spurious eigenmodes and preclude the use of efficient Hermitian eigensolvers.
    \item How should boundary conditions and intersections between local subdomains and physical domain boundaries be treated without introducing singular boundary integrals?
\end{enumerate}

In this paper, we resolve all these challenges by formulating, implementing, and validating the **VNMM-MLPG**—a truly cell-free vector nodal meshless method for 2D electromagnetics. By adopting the Local Galerkin variation (MLPG6) \cite{atluri2002meshless}, where vector test functions coincide with the local trial basis within each independent disk $\Omega_s^I$, we obtain:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Closed-Form Analytical Integration:} Exact closed-form integration of all local stiffness and mass matrices over the disk subdomains, completely eliminating numerical quadrature error.
    \item \textbf{Exact Symmetry and Positive-Semidefiniteness:} Both global matrices $\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{M}$ are strictly symmetric and positive-(semi)definite, guaranteeing real physical spectra.
    \item \textbf{Direct Tangential PEC Collocation:} Elimination of degrees of freedom on perfectly conducting boundaries bypasses boundary integrals over truncated subdomains.
    \item \textbf{High Computational Efficiency:} Assembly is over $3.5\times$ faster and generates matrices that are $36\%$ sparser than conventional cell-based VNMM-Gauss schemes.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section~\ref{sec:formulation} details the mathematical formulation of VNMM-MLPG, proves the breakdown of classical weight test functions, derives the closed-form Local Galerkin (MLPG6) matrices, and discusses local conformity versus global non-conformity. Section~\ref{sec:validation_methodology} outlines the four-stage validation roadmap. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the numerical results: machine-precision patch test satisfaction, MMS asymptotic convergence rates, resonant cavity eigenvalue spectra and spectral purification, and computational benchmarking. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses advantages, current limitations, and future directions. Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes the paper.

\section{Theoretical Formulation of the VNMM-MLPG}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Continuous Boundary-Value Problem}
\label{subsec:continuous_bvp}

Consider a bounded two-dimensional domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ with a Lipschitz boundary $\partial\Omega = \Gamma_{\mathrm{PEC}} \cup \Gamma_{\mathrm{abs}}$, occupied by a linear, isotropic, non-dispersive medium characterized by relative permeability $\mu_r$ and permittivity $\epsilon_r$. The time-harmonic electric field $\mathbf{E}(x, y) = [E_x, E_y]^T$ satisfies Maxwell's curl-curl equation. To eliminate gradient-type spurious modes on non-conforming meshless spaces, we adopt the div-curl regularized vector Helmholtz equation:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla \times \left(\frac{1}{\mu_r} \nabla \times \mathbf{E}\right) - \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mu_r} \nabla(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}) - k_0^2 \epsilon_r \mathbf{E} = \mathbf{J}_s \quad \text{in } \Omega,
    \label{eq:helmholtz_pde}
\end{equation}
subject to the homogeneous Dirichlet condition on perfectly conducting (PEC) boundaries:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{n} \times \mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0} \quad \iff \quad \mathbf{E} \cdot \mathbf{t} = 0 \quad \text{on } \Gamma_{\mathrm{PEC}},
    \label{eq:pec_bc}
\end{equation}
where $k_0 = \omega\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}$ is the free-space wavenumber, $s_{\mathrm{div}} \ge 0$ is the dimensionless divergence regularization parameter, $\mathbf{J}_s$ is an applied electric current density, and $\mathbf{n}$, $\mathbf{t}$ denote the outward unit normal and unit tangent to $\partial\Omega$, respectively. In two dimensions with in-plane vector fields $\mathbf{E} = [E_x, E_y]^T$, the differential operators are defined by:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla \times \mathbf{E} = \left(\frac{\partial E_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial E_x}{\partial y}\right) \hat{\mathbf{z}}, \qquad \nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\partial E_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial y}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Nodal Discretization and Local Support Geometries}
\label{subsec:nodal_discretization}

The domain $\overline{\Omega} = \Omega \cup \partial\Omega$ is discretized by a cloud of $N$ nodes $\mathcal{S} = \{P_I\}_{I=1}^N$ with Cartesian coordinates $\mathbf{x}_I = [x_I, y_I]^T$. Each node $P_I$ is endowed with an assigned unit director vector $\mathbf{t}_I = [t_{x,I}, t_{y,I}]^T$, defining a scalar degree of freedom (DoF):
\begin{equation}
    e_I = \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}_I) \cdot \mathbf{t}_I.
\end{equation}
On PEC boundaries ($\mathbf{x}_I \in \Gamma_{\mathrm{PEC}}$), directors are oriented strictly tangential to the wall ($\mathbf{t}_I \parallel \partial\Omega$), so that the physical condition \eqref{eq:pec_bc} translates directly into the homogeneous constraint $e_I = 0$.

In the MLPG framework, two distinct local domains are defined for each node $P_I$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic_mlpg}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Trial Support Domain ($\Omega_u^I$):} The spatial neighborhood containing an admissible stencil of $K = 6$ nodes used to construct the local trial vector field $\mathbf{E}^h(\mathbf{x})$.
    \item \textbf{Local Integration Subdomain ($\Omega_s^I$):} A circular disk of radius $r_s^I$ centered at $\mathbf{x}_I$:
    \begin{equation}
        \Omega_s^I = \left\{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^2 : \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_I\| \le r_s^I \right\}, \qquad r_s^I = \alpha_s h_I,
        \label{eq:subdomain_disk}
    \end{equation}
    where $h_I$ is the local nodal spacing (distance to nearest neighbor), and $\alpha_s \in [0.7, 1.2]$ is a dimensionless radius scaling parameter.
\end{enumerate}

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{fig_mlpg_subdomains_schematic.png}
    \caption{Schematic of the VNMM-MLPG discretization on a 2D domain $\Omega = [0, \pi]^2$. For an interior node $\mathbf{x}_I$, the circular local integration subdomain $\Omega_s^I$ (cyan dashed disk) is completely decoupled from global background grids. The trial interpolation stencil $\Omega_u^I$ consists of 6 admissible neighbor nodes connected by dotted lines. On the boundary, $\Omega_s^B$ intersects $\partial\Omega$, where the tangential director $\mathbf{t}_B \parallel \partial\Omega$ directly enforces the PEC condition $e_B = 0$.}

\end{figure}

\subsection{Complete Linear Trial Space ($\mathcal{P}^1$)}
\label{subsec:trial_space}

Following \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}, within the influence zone of node $P_I$, the vector field $\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x})$ is approximated in the complete linear polynomial space $\mathcal{P}_1 \times \mathcal{P}_1$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{E}^h(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{a}_0 + \mathbf{a}_1 (x - x_I) + \mathbf{a}_2 (y - y_I) = \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_I) \mathbf{a},
    \label{eq:polynomial_expansion}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{a} = [a_{x0}, a_{y0}, a_{x1}, a_{y1}, a_{x2}, a_{y2}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^6$ is a vector of expansion coefficients, and:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{P}(\Delta\mathbf{x}) = \begin{bmatrix}
        1 & 0 & \Delta x & 0 & \Delta y & 0 \\
        0 & 1 & 0 & \Delta x & 0 & \Delta y
    \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 6}.
\end{equation}
Collocating the directional projections $e_k = \mathbf{E}^h(\mathbf{x}_k) \cdot \mathbf{t}_k$ at the 6 stencil nodes of $\Omega_u^I$ yields the linear system:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}_I \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{stencil}}, \qquad A_{k, :} = \mathbf{t}_k^T \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{x}_I) \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 6}.
    \label{eq:collocation_system}
\end{equation}
To ensure well-conditioned stencil selection, candidate sextets are queried via a $k$-d tree and filtered using the scale-invariant determinant metric:
\begin{equation}
    |\det(\mathbf{A}_I)| \ge \mathrm{Tol}_{\mathrm{det}}(h) = \mathrm{Tol}_{\mathrm{ref}} \left(\frac{h_I}{h_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)^4,
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{Tol}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 10^{-4}$ and $h_{\mathrm{ref}} = \pi/20$. Inverting $\mathbf{A}_I$ gives $\mathbf{a} = \boldsymbol{\beta}_I \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{stencil}}$, where $\boldsymbol{\beta}_I = \mathbf{A}_I^{-1} \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$. The discrete field and its vector shape functions $\mathbf{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x})$ are then expressed as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{E}^h(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{a=1}^6 \mathbf{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) e_a, \qquad \mathbf{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_I) \boldsymbol{\beta}_{:, a}.
    \label{eq:shape_functions}
\end{equation}
Remarkably, because $\mathbf{N}_a^I$ is linear in Cartesian coordinates, its curl and divergence are \textbf{strictly constant} everywhere inside $\Omega_s^I$:
\begin{align}
    (\nabla \times \mathbf{N}_a^I)_z &= \beta_{3, a} - \beta_{4, a} \equiv c_{\mathrm{curl}, a}^I \in \mathbb{R}, \label{eq:curl_constant} \\
    \nabla \cdot \mathbf{N}_a^I &= \beta_{2, a} + \beta_{5, a} \equiv c_{\mathrm{div}, a}^I \in \mathbb{R}. \label{eq:div_constant}
\end{align}

\subsection{Local Weak Form and the Vector Test Function Paradox}
\label{subsec:local_weak_form}

Multiplying \eqref{eq:helmholtz_pde} by an arbitrary vector test function $\mathbf{W}_I(\mathbf{x})$ supported on the local subdomain $\Omega_s^I$ and integrating over $\Omega_s^I$ yields the local variational Petrov--Galerkin equation:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \iint_{\Omega_s^I} \bigg[ \frac{1}{\mu_r} (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{W}_I) &+ \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mu_r} (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}) (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{W}_I) \\
        &- k_0^2 \epsilon_r \mathbf{E} \cdot \mathbf{W}_I \bigg] dA + B_{\partial\Omega_s^I} = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} \mathbf{J}_s \cdot \mathbf{W}_I dA,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:local_weak_form}
\end{equation}
where the boundary flux term $B_{\partial\Omega_s^I}$ incorporates integration-by-parts boundary fluxes along $\partial\Omega_s^I$:
\begin{equation}
    B_{\partial\Omega_s^I} = -\oint_{\partial\Omega_s^I} \frac{1}{\mu_r} \left( \mathbf{n} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) \right) \cdot \mathbf{W}_I \, ds - \oint_{\partial\Omega_s^I} \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mu_r} (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}) (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{W}_I) \, ds.
    \label{eq:boundary_flux}
\end{equation}

In scalar MLPG formulations (e.g., MLPG1 \cite{atluri1998new}), the test function is chosen as a smooth radial weight function $w_I(r)$ that vanishes continuously on $\partial\Omega_s^I$ ($w_I(r_s^I) = 0$). For vector fields, a natural first intuition would be to choose $\mathbf{W}_I(\mathbf{x}) = w_I(r) \mathbf{t}_I$, where $w_I(r)$ is a $C^1$ quartic spline:
\begin{equation}
    w_I(r) = \begin{cases}
        1 - 6\left(\frac{r}{r_s}\right)^2 + 8\left(\frac{r}{r_s}\right)^3 - 3\left(\frac{r}{r_s}\right)^4, & r \le r_s, \\
        0, & r > r_s.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:quartic_spline}
\end{equation}
Because $w_I(r_s) = 0$ on $\partial\Omega_s^I$, the boundary flux $B_{\partial\Omega_s^I}$ in \eqref{eq:boundary_flux} vanishes identically. However, this classical choice leads to a severe mathematical collapse when applied to complete linear vector polynomials, as formalized in the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Breakdown of MLPG1 for Linear Vector Fields]
\label{thm:mlpg1_collapse}
Let $\Omega_s \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be a circular disk of radius $r_s$, and let $\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{x}) = w(r) \mathbf{v}_0$ be a vector test function where $\mathbf{v}_0$ is a constant vector and $w(r)$ is a continuously differentiable radial weight function satisfying $w(r_s) = 0$ on $\partial\Omega_s$. Then, for ANY linear vector trial field $\mathbf{E} \in \mathcal{P}_1 \times \mathcal{P}_1$, the stiffness bilinear forms vanish identically:
\begin{align}
    \iint_{\Omega_s} (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{W}) \, dA &\equiv 0, \label{eq:curl_zero} \\
    \iint_{\Omega_s} (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}) (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{W}) \, dA &\equiv 0. \label{eq:div_zero}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any linear field $\mathbf{E} \in \mathcal{P}_1 \times \mathcal{P}_1$, its curl and divergence are constants on $\Omega_s$:
\begin{equation}
    (\nabla \times \mathbf{E})_z = C_{\mathrm{curl}} \in \mathbb{R}, \qquad \nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = C_{\mathrm{div}} \in \mathbb{R}.
\end{equation}
Factoring these constants out of the integrals:
\begin{equation}
    \iint_{\Omega_s} (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{W}) \, dA = C_{\mathrm{curl}} \iint_{\Omega_s} (\nabla \times \mathbf{W})_z \, dA.
\end{equation}
Applying Stokes' circulation theorem on the disk $\Omega_s$:
\begin{equation}
    \iint_{\Omega_s} (\nabla \times \mathbf{W})_z \, dA = \oint_{\partial\Omega_s} \mathbf{W} \cdot \mathbf{t}_{\mathrm{circ}} \, ds = \oint_{\partial\Omega_s} w(r_s) (\mathbf{v}_0 \cdot \mathbf{t}_{\mathrm{circ}}) \, ds.
\end{equation}
Since $w(r_s) = 0$ everywhere on $\partial\Omega_s$, the circulation integral is identically zero, proving \eqref{eq:curl_zero}.

Similarly, for the divergence term:
\begin{equation}
    \iint_{\Omega_s} (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}) (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{W}) \, dA = C_{\mathrm{div}} \iint_{\Omega_s} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{W} \, dA.
\end{equation}
Applying the Gauss divergence theorem:
\begin{equation}
    \iint_{\Omega_s} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{W} \, dA = \oint_{\partial\Omega_s} \mathbf{W} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, ds = \oint_{\partial\Omega_s} w(r_s) (\mathbf{v}_0 \cdot \mathbf{n}) \, ds \equiv 0.
\end{equation}
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:mlpg1_collapse} reveals that using standard MLPG1 weight test functions on complete linear vector fields yields an identically zero stiffness matrix ($\mathbf{K} \equiv \mathbf{0}$)! Any attempt to circumvent this by avoiding integration by parts reintroduces boundary traction integrals involving high-order derivatives, which are unavailable in $C^0$ meshless interpolants.

\subsection{Justification and Selection of MLPG6 (Local Galerkin)}
\label{subsec:mlpg6_selection}

To establish a mathematically consistent vector formulation, we examine the six MLPG variants classified by Atluri and Shen \cite{atluri2002meshless}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MLPG1 (Weight function as test):} Collapses identically for linear fields by Theorem~\ref{thm:mlpg1_collapse}.
    \item \textbf{MLPG2 (Point collocation):} $\mathbf{W}_I = \delta(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_I)$. Evaluates strong-form PDEs directly at nodes, requiring second-order spatial derivatives and suffering from numerical instability on irregular clouds.
    \item \textbf{MLPG3 (Discrete least squares):} Minimizes PDE residuals, but squares the matrix condition number ($\mathrm{cond}(\mathbf{K}) \sim \mathcal{O}(h^{-4})$), creating severe algebraic conditioning challenges.
    \item \textbf{MLPG4 (Partition of unity):} Requires constructing global partitions of unity, which reintroduces background geometric complexity.
    \item \textbf{MLPG5 (Local boundary integral / Heaviside):} Employs $\mathbf{W}_I = \mathbf{v}_0$ (constant). Eliminates domain integrals of stiffness, but requires evaluating line integrals of normal and tangential field derivatives along $\partial\Omega_s^I$, which are highly sensitive to inter-stencil trace jumps.
    \item \textbf{MLPG6 (Local Galerkin Method):} The vector test functions are chosen to be the \textbf{trial shape functions themselves} within each local subdomain:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{W}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) \quad \forall \mathbf{x} \in \Omega_s^I, \quad a = 1, \dots, 6.
        \label{eq:mlpg6_choice}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

The Local Galerkin choice (MLPG6) possesses decisive mathematical advantages for VNMM:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Non-Trivial Consistency:} Since $\nabla \times \mathbf{N}_a^I$ and $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{N}_a^I$ are non-zero constants, their inner products against themselves are strictly positive.
    \item \textbf{Exact Symmetry and Positive-Semidefiniteness:} The local bilinear form over $\Omega_s^I$ is symmetric. Scattering local $6 \times 6$ blocks into the global system yields strictly symmetric global stiffness and mass matrices:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{K} = \mathbf{K}^T \ge 0, \qquad \mathbf{M} = \mathbf{M}^T > 0.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Real Spectrum Guarantee:} Preserving Hermitian symmetry guarantees that all eigenvalues of the generalized problem $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} \mathbf{c} = \lambda \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{red}} \mathbf{c}$ are strictly real ($\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$), precluding complex spurious modes.
    \item \textbf{Exact Closed-Form Integration:} The polynomial integrands on the circular disk can be evaluated analytically in closed form, completely eliminating quadrature errors.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Closed-Form Analytical Discretization Formulas}
\label{subsec:analytic_formulas}

Let $\Omega_s^I$ be a circular disk of radius $r_s$. Its area and polar second moment of area are:
\begin{equation}
    S_0 = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} dA = \pi r_s^2, \qquad S_2 = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (x - x_I)^2 dA = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (y - y_I)^2 dA = \frac{\pi r_s^4}{4}.
    \label{eq:disk_moments}
\end{equation}
All odd moments vanish identically by radial symmetry:
\begin{equation}
    \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (x - x_I) \, dA = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (y - y_I) \, dA = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (x - x_I)(y - y_I) \, dA \equiv 0.
\end{equation}

Substituting the trial functions \eqref{eq:shape_functions} into the local weak form, the local stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}} \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$ is partitioned into curl and divergence contributions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}} = \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{curl}}^{\mathrm{local}} + s_{\mathrm{div}} \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{div}}^{\mathrm{local}},
\end{equation}
where, using the constant vectors $\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I, \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I \in \mathbb{R}^6$ defined in \eqref{eq:curl_constant}--\eqref{eq:div_constant}:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{curl}}^{\mathrm{local}} &= \frac{1}{\mu_r} \left( \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I \otimes \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I \right) \pi r_s^2, \label{eq:k_curl_analytic} \\
    \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{div}}^{\mathrm{local}} &= \frac{1}{\mu_r} \left( \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I \otimes \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I \right) \pi r_s^2. \label{eq:k_div_analytic}
\end{align}
Both matrices are outer products of vectors with themselves, hence manifestly symmetric and positive-semidefinite.

For the local mass matrix $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}} \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$:
\begin{equation}
    M_{I, ab}^{\mathrm{local}} = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} \epsilon_r (\mathbf{N}_a^I \cdot \mathbf{N}_b^I) \, dA.
\end{equation}
Expanding the dot product using the polynomial basis:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{N}_b^I(\mathbf{x}) = \boldsymbol{\beta}_{0:2, a}^T \boldsymbol{\beta}_{0:2, b} + \boldsymbol{\beta}_{2:6, a}^T \boldsymbol{\beta}_{2:6, b} \left[ (x - x_I)^2 + (y - y_I)^2 \right] + \text{odd terms}.
\end{equation}
Integrating over the disk yields the closed-form mass matrix:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}} = \epsilon_r \left( \mathbf{C}_0 \pi r_s^2 + \mathbf{C}_1 \frac{\pi r_s^4}{4} \right),
    \label{eq:m_local_analytic}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{C}_0 = \boldsymbol{\beta}_{0:2}^T \boldsymbol{\beta}_{0:2} \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$ and $\mathbf{C}_1 = \boldsymbol{\beta}_{2:6}^T \boldsymbol{\beta}_{2:6} \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$. Both $\mathbf{C}_0$ and $\mathbf{C}_1$ are Gram matrices of real submatrices, guaranteeing that $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}}$ is strictly symmetric and positive-definite.

Global assembly proceeds by summing all local contributions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K} = \sum_{I=1}^N \mathbf{L}_I^T \mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}} \mathbf{L}_I, \qquad \mathbf{M} = \sum_{I=1}^N \mathbf{L}_I^T \mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}} \mathbf{L}_I,
    \label{eq:global_assembly}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{L}_I \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times N}$ is the Boolean gathering matrix mapping the 6 stencil nodes of $\Omega_u^I$ to global DoF indices.

\subsection{Analysis and Treatment of Boundary Conditions}
\label{subsec:boundary_treatment}

A well-known challenge in meshless methods, particularly within the MLPG paradigm, is the enforcement of boundary conditions and the treatment of boundary subdomains $\Omega_s^B$ that intersect the physical domain boundary $\partial\Omega$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic_mlpg}). In conventional scalar meshless methods based on Moving Least Squares (MLS) or radial basis functions (RBF), shape functions lack the Kronecker-delta property ($\phi_I(\mathbf{x}_J) \ne \delta_{IJ}$), requiring complex external mechanisms such as Lagrange multipliers, penalty parameters, or boundary element coupling \cite{atluri2002meshless}.

In the VNMM-MLPG framework, the directional vector degree of freedom $e_k = \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}_k) \cdot \mathbf{t}_k$ combined with local weak-form integration yields an exceptionally clean, versatile structure for all standard electromagnetic boundary conditions. Consider a general partition of the physical boundary $\partial\Omega = \Gamma_D \cup \Gamma_N \cup \Gamma_R$, where $\Gamma_D$ denotes Dirichlet boundaries (electric walls / PEC), $\Gamma_N$ denotes Neumann boundaries (magnetic walls / PMC / surface current sheets), and $\Gamma_R$ denotes Robin boundaries (impedance / absorbing radiation).

For any node $\mathbf{x}_I$ whose local subdomain $\Omega_s^I$ intersects the physical boundary $\partial\Omega$, the boundary of the local integration disk splits into an interior circular arc $L_s^I = \partial\Omega_s^I \setminus \partial\Omega$ and a boundary intersection $\Gamma_s^I = \partial\Omega_s^I \cap \partial\Omega$:
\begin{equation}
    \partial\Omega_s^I = L_s^I \cup \Gamma_s^I, \qquad \Gamma_s^I = (\partial\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_D) \cup (\partial\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_N) \cup (\partial\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_R).
\end{equation}
Integration by parts in the local weak form \eqref{eq:local_weak_form} yields the boundary flux line integral:
\begin{equation}
    \oint_{\partial\Omega_s^I} \boldsymbol{\tau} \cdot \mathbf{W}_I \, ds = \int_{L_s^I} \boldsymbol{\tau} \cdot \mathbf{W}_I \, ds + \int_{\Gamma_s^I} \boldsymbol{\tau} \cdot \mathbf{W}_I \, ds,
    \label{eq:boundary_flux_partition}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ is the generalized electromagnetic traction vector along outward normal $\mathbf{n}$:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau} = \frac{1}{\mu_r} \left( \mathbf{n} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) \right) + \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mu_r} (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}) \mathbf{n}.
    \label{eq:traction_vector}
\end{equation}
We now analyze each boundary condition type in detail.

\subsubsection{Inhomogeneous and Homogeneous Dirichlet Boundary Conditions}
\label{subsubsec:dirichlet_bc}

On a Dirichlet boundary $\Gamma_D$, the tangential trace of the electric field is prescribed:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{n} \times \mathbf{E} = \mathbf{g}_D \quad \iff \quad E_t(\mathbf{x}) = g_D(\mathbf{x}) \quad \forall \mathbf{x} \in \Gamma_D.
    \label{eq:dirichlet_prescribed}
\end{equation}
In the VNMM, the tangential director $\mathbf{t}_k$ of each boundary node $\mathbf{x}_k \in \Gamma_D$ is aligned strictly parallel to the boundary tangent ($\mathbf{t}_k \parallel \Gamma_D$). Because the VNMM trial space satisfies the exact directional nodal projection property at the node itself:
\begin{equation}
    e_k = \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}_k) \cdot \mathbf{t}_k = g_D(\mathbf{x}_k).
    \label{eq:dirichlet_nodal_value}
\end{equation}
Thus, essential Dirichlet conditions are satisfied directly at the discrete nodal level without any Lagrange multipliers or penalty parameters. Partitioning the global DoF vector into active interior DoFs $\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{int}}$ and prescribed boundary DoFs $\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{bnd}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ii}} & \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ib}} \\
        \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{bi}} & \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{bb}}
    \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{int}} \\
        \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{bnd}}
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{int}} \\
        \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{bnd}}
    \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
the exact boundary values $\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{bnd}} = [g_D(\mathbf{x}_k)]$ are substituted directly into the system, yielding the condensed, strictly symmetric positive-definite linear system:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ii}} \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{int}} = \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{int}} - \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ib}} \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{bnd}}.
    \label{eq:dirichlet_condensed_system}
\end{equation}
For homogeneous Dirichlet boundaries (perfect electric conductor, PEC, $\mathbf{g}_D \equiv \mathbf{0}$), $\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{bnd}} = \mathbf{0}$, reducing the source problem to $\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ii}} \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{int}} = \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{int}}$. Similarly, in resonant cavity eigenvalue problems, setting $\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{bnd}} = \mathbf{0}$ yields the condensed generalized eigenvalue problem:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{int}} = \lambda \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{red}} \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{int}}, \qquad \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} = \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{ii}}, \quad \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{red}} = \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{ii}}.
    \label{eq:reduced_eigenproblem}
\end{equation}
Because boundary DoFs are eliminated directly, evaluating boundary integrals along $\Gamma_D$ is avoided entirely.

\subsubsection{Homogeneous Neumann Boundary Conditions (Natural / PMC / Symmetry)}
\label{subsubsec:neumann_homogeneous}

On a homogeneous Neumann boundary $\Gamma_N$ (such as a perfect magnetic conductor, PMC, or a plane of electromagnetic geometric symmetry), the natural boundary condition requires the tangential magnetic field or surface curl traction to vanish:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{n} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) = \mathbf{0} \quad \text{on } \Gamma_N.
    \label{eq:neumann_homogeneous_pde}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for physical solenoidal fields satisfying the divergence-free condition in source-free regions, $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = 0$. Substituting these into the traction vector \eqref{eq:traction_vector} shows that:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau} \equiv \mathbf{0} \quad \text{on } \Gamma_N.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the physical boundary flux integral over $\Gamma_s^I \subset \Gamma_N$ vanishes identically:
\begin{equation}
    \int_{\partial\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_N} \boldsymbol{\tau} \cdot \mathbf{W}_I \, ds \equiv 0.
\end{equation}
This establishes a crucial theoretical advantage of the VNMM-MLPG: \textbf{homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions are strictly natural}. No line integrals, no boundary segment quadratures, and no boundary corrections are needed. Nodes located on $\Gamma_N$ are simply treated as active, unconstrained degrees of freedom belonging to $\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{int}}$. Their equations are assembled normally from their local subdomains $\Omega_s^I$, and their tangential fields are resolved automatically as part of the global system solution.

\subsubsection{Inhomogeneous Neumann Boundary Conditions (Surface Currents)}
\label{subsubsec:neumann_inhomogeneous}

When an inhomogeneous Neumann condition is prescribed on $\Gamma_N$, representing an impressed surface magnetic current density $\mathbf{M}_s$ or a non-zero magnetic tangential traction:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{\mu_r} \left( \mathbf{n} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) \right) = \mathbf{g}_N(\mathbf{x}) \quad \text{on } \Gamma_N,
    \label{eq:neumann_inhomogeneous_pde}
\end{equation}
the traction vector becomes $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{g}_N(\mathbf{x})$ (under $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = 0$). In the local weak form for any node whose subdomain intersects $\Gamma_N$, this boundary traction introduces a non-zero line integral over the intersection segment $\Gamma_s^I = \partial\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_N$:
\begin{equation}
    F_a^{\mathrm{Neumann}, I} = \int_{\Gamma_s^I} \mathbf{g}_N(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{W}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) \, ds = \int_{\Gamma_s^I} \mathbf{g}_N(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) \, ds.
    \label{eq:neumann_load_integral}
\end{equation}
In the Local Galerkin formulation (MLPG6), the test basis $\mathbf{W}_a^I = \mathbf{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x})$ is a complete linear vector polynomial. For planar boundaries (such as straight cavity walls or polygonal waveguides), the intersection $\Gamma_s^I$ is a simple straight line segment $[-L_0, L_0]$. Because the test functions are linear polynomials in Cartesian coordinates, the line integral \eqref{eq:neumann_load_integral} can be evaluated either in closed analytical form or via a standard 2-point 1D Gauss--Legendre quadrature. The resulting vector $\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{Neumann}, I}$ is added directly to the local load vector, fully maintaining the cell-free character of the method.

\subsubsection{Mixed Boundary Conditions and Robin / Impedance Boundaries}
\label{subsubsec:mixed_robin_bc}

In general computational domains, mixed boundary conditions are enforced by partitioning the boundary into complementary sub-domains $\partial\Omega = \Gamma_D \cup \Gamma_N \cup \Gamma_R$ with $\Gamma_D \cap \Gamma_N = \emptyset$. At transition corners where a Dirichlet wall meets a Neumann wall, Dirichlet conditions take precedence, fixing the corner DoF, while adjacent interior nodes on the Neumann segment remain free.

Furthermore, first-order absorbing boundary conditions (ABC) or impedance boundary conditions on $\Gamma_R$ are expressed in Robin form:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{\mu_r} \left( \mathbf{n} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) \right) + \gamma \mathbf{E}_t = \mathbf{g}_R(\mathbf{x}) \quad \text{on } \Gamma_R,
    \label{eq:robin_pde}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma = j k_0 \sqrt{\epsilon_r / \mu_r}$ represents the surface admittance and $\mathbf{E}_t = \mathbf{n} \times (\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{n})$ is the tangential electric field vector. Substituting \eqref{eq:robin_pde} into the local boundary flux \eqref{eq:boundary_flux_partition} shifts the dependent field term $\gamma \mathbf{E}_t$ to the bilinear form, yielding a local boundary damping matrix:
\begin{equation}
    C_{ab}^{\mathrm{Robin}, I} = \gamma \int_{\partial\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_R} \left( \mathbf{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{t} \right) \left( \mathbf{N}_b^I(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{t} \right) \, ds,
    \label{eq:robin_matrix}
\end{equation}
while the independent term $\mathbf{g}_R$ contributes to the right-hand load vector. Because $\partial\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_R$ is a 1D straight or circular boundary segment, \eqref{eq:robin_matrix} is readily integrated using 1D Gauss quadrature without requiring background domain meshes.

\subsection{Local Conformity vs. Global Non-Conformity}
\label{subsec:conformity_discussion}

A central conceptual question in vector meshless methods concerns conformity:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Local Conformity:} Within each local subdomain $\Omega_s^I$, the trial function $\mathbf{E}^h(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_I) \boldsymbol{\beta}_I \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{stencil}}$ is a smooth, continuous vector polynomial $\in C^\infty(\Omega_s^I) \subset H(\mathbf{curl}, \Omega_s^I)$. Inside $\Omega_s^I$, there are no internal edges, no jump discontinuities, and no singular distributions.
    \item \textbf{Global Non-Conformity:} Because each node $P_I$ independently selects its own trial stencil $\Omega_u^I$, evaluating $\mathbf{E}^h(\mathbf{x})$ across overlapping support domains produces inter-stencil trace jumps across shifting support boundaries. Consequently, the global discrete space does not strictly conform to $H(\mathbf{curl}, \Omega)$.
\end{itemize}

In conventional cell-based VNMM-Gauss, this global non-conformity caused serious numerical instability: when Gauss integration points sampled intra-cell stencil transitions, derivative jumps contaminated the global curl operator, requiring dense over-integration to suppress rank deficiencies. In contrast, the **VNMM-MLPG resolves this tension by localizing integration**:
1. Integration is performed strictly over autonomous circular disks $\Omega_s^I$.
2. Inside each disk $\Omega_s^I$, the trial stencil is **fixed and invariant** (the 6 nodes of $\Omega_u^I$).
3. Therefore, the integrand is perfectly smooth ($C^\infty$) across the entire integration domain $\Omega_s^I$, completely eliminating intra-domain jump discontinuities.
4. As the nodal spacing $h \to 0$, inter-stencil discrepancies between adjacent overlapping disks decay asymptotically at $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$, ensuring asymptotic consistency and convergence.

\section{Validation Methodology}
\label{sec:validation_methodology}

To establish the mathematical consistency, asymptotic convergence, and physical fidelity of the VNMM-MLPG, we construct a four-stage verification roadmap:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Element-Level Analytical Verification.} Verify that closed-form analytical formulas \eqref{eq:k_curl_analytic}--\eqref{eq:m_local_analytic} match high-precision polar Gauss quadrature ($n_r = 6, n_\theta = 16$) to machine tolerance ($\le 10^{-10}$).
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Galerkin Patch Test for Linear Consistency.} Verify that on both regular grids and irregular point clouds with $25\%$ stochastic position jitter, the discrete system reproduces all seven canonical affine vector states to machine precision:
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{\|\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{int}} - \mathbf{e}^*\|_{\infty}}{\|\mathbf{e}^*\|_{\infty}} \le 10^{-11}.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Stage 3: Method of Manufactured Solutions (MMS) under Dirichlet and Neumann Conditions.} Verify asymptotic convergence orders under spatial refinement ($h \to 0$) for an inhomogeneous vector Helmholtz problem with exact solution $\mathbf{E}^* = [\sin(x)\cos(y), -\cos(x)\sin(y)]^T$ under both:
    \begin{enumerate}[(a)]
        \item Pure essential Dirichlet boundary conditions on all four physical walls ($\partial\Omega$).
        \item Mixed Dirichlet--Neumann boundary conditions, where inhomogeneous Dirichlet traces are imposed on $x \in \{0, \pi\}$ and natural homogeneous Neumann conditions ($\mathbf{n} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) = \mathbf{0}$) are prescribed on $y \in \{0, \pi\}$ with boundary nodes treated as active free unknowns.
    \end{enumerate}
    The optimal theoretical polynomial rates must be recovered in both configurations:
    \begin{equation}
        \|\mathbf{E}^h - \mathbf{E}^*\|_{L^2} \sim \mathcal{O}(h^2), \qquad \|(\nabla \times \mathbf{E}^h)_z - (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}^*)_z\|_{L^2} \sim \mathcal{O}(h).
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Stage 4: Physical Spectral Validation on a 2D PEC Cavity.} Solve the generalized eigenvalue problem $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} \mathbf{c} = k_c^2 \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{red}} \mathbf{c}$ on a square resonant cavity $\Omega = [0, \pi]^2$. Compare cutoff wavenumbers $k_c$ for the first 10 physical transverse electric (TE) modes against the benchmark analytical solutions tabulated by Ortiz (2023) \cite{ortiz2023tese}, assess spectral purification via the divergence regularization parameter $s_{\mathrm{div}}$, and benchmark computational performance against cell-based VNMM-Gauss.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 1: Element-Level Closed-Form Verification}
\label{subsec:stage1_results}

The closed-form analytical formulas \eqref{eq:k_curl_analytic}--\eqref{eq:m_local_analytic} were verified against polar numerical quadrature on a disk $\Omega_s$ ($r_s = 0.35$ m, $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 4.0$). \end{align}
This confirms that the analytical expressions evaluate the exact polynomial integrals on circular disks down to double-precision machine epsilon, eliminating numerical quadrature error from the MLPG formulation.

\subsection{Stage 3: Asymptotic Refinement Convergence via MMS (Dirichlet and Neumann)}
\label{subsec:stage3_mms}

To assess asymptotic convergence rates under spatial refinement ($h \to 0$), the Method of Manufactured Solutions (MMS) was solved on $\Omega = [0, \pi]^2$ with exact analytical vector field:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{E}^*(x, y) = \begin{bmatrix} \sin(x) \cos(y) \\ -\cos(x) \sin(y) \end{bmatrix}, \qquad (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}^*)_z = 2\sin(x)\sin(y), \quad \nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}^* = 0.
\end{equation}
The regularized vector Helmholtz equation \eqref{eq:helmholtz_pde} was solved with $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 6.0$, mass parameter $\kappa = 1.0$, and volumetric source $\mathbf{J}_s = (2 + \kappa) \mathbf{E}^*$. Refinement was evaluated across a sequence of five uniform point clouds with $N \in \{9 \times 9, 13 \times 13, 17 \times 17, 21 \times 21, 25 \times 25\}$, corresponding to characteristic nodal spacings $h = \pi/(N-1) \in [0.3927, 0.1309]$ rad.

To systematically test the boundary formulation developed in Section~\ref{subsec:boundary_treatment}, two distinct boundary-value problem configurations were investigated:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Case A (Pure Inhomogeneous Dirichlet):} On all four boundary walls $\partial\Omega$, the tangential trace is prescribed by the exact manufactured field ($e_k = \mathbf{E}^*(\mathbf{x}_k) \cdot \mathbf{t}_k$), and all boundary nodes are eliminated via the condensed system \eqref{eq:dirichlet_condensed_system}.
    \item \textbf{Case B (Mixed Dirichlet--Neumann / PMC):} Inhomogeneous Dirichlet conditions are imposed on the vertical walls $x \in \{0, \pi\}$, while the horizontal walls $y \in \{0, \pi\}$ are treated as homogeneous natural Neumann (PMC) boundaries. Because $(\nabla \times \mathbf{E}^*)_z = 2\sin(x)\sin(y)$ vanishes identically along $y=0$ and $y=\pi$, the exact electromagnetic traction satisfies $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \frac{1}{\mu_r} (\mathbf{n} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}^*)) \equiv \mathbf{0}$. Therefore, the boundary nodes along $y \in \{0, \pi\}$ are treated as active, unconstrained interior-type degrees of freedom and determined directly by the global linear system.
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}

\begin{equation}
    k_{c, nm} = \sqrt{\lambda_{nm}} = \sqrt{n^2 + m^2}, \qquad n, m \in \mathbb{N}_0, \quad (n, m) \ne (0, 0).
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    R_{\mathrm{div}} = \frac{\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{div}} \mathbf{v}}{\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{curl}} \mathbf{v}} \times 100\%.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Current Deficiencies and Open Challenges}
\label{subsec:deficiencies_challenges}

Despite its proven efficacy, several open questions remain for the VNMM-MLPG:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Higher-Order Cavity Mode Dispersion:} As observed in Table~\ref{tab:cavity_eigenvalues}, higher-order cavity modes (such as $TE_{22}$ with error $15.7\%$) exhibit larger errors than lower modes ($TE_{10}$ at $0.47\%$). This behavior stems from the linear polynomial capacity of $\mathcal{P}^1$: capturing modes with multiple spatial wavelengths per domain dimension requires denser point clouds ($h \ll \lambda_{\mathrm{wavelength}} / 10$).
    \item \textbf{Dielectric and Material Interfaces:} In multi-medium domains with discontinuous permittivity $\epsilon_r$ or permeability $\mu_r$, normal electric fields exhibit step jumps while tangential fields remain continuous. Developing interface-aligned director orientations and discontinuous local stencils is an essential avenue for future research.
    \item \textbf{Extension to Three Dimensions:} The mathematical structure derived herein extends naturally to 3D by replacing circular disks with spheres of radius $r_s$. In 3D, the complete linear vector basis $\mathcal{P}_1^3$ contains 12 terms, and spherical moments of area evaluate analytically to $V_0 = \frac{4}{3}\pi r_s^3$ and $I_2 = \frac{4}{15}\pi r_s^5$. Implementing this closed-form 3D extension constitutes the immediate next phase of this project.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This work presented the formulation, implementation, and rigorous validation of the first truly cell-free Vector Nodal Meshless Method via Meshless Local Petrov--Galerkin (VNMM-MLPG) for two-dimensional computational electromagnetics. By decoupling spatial integration from global background grids and assigning autonomous circular subdomains $\Omega_s^I$ to each node, the method eliminates intra-cell stencil switching and quadrature softening. We demonstrated analytically that classical weight test functions (MLPG1) collapse on linear vector fields due to Stokes' and Gauss' theorems, justifying the adoption of the Local Galerkin variation (MLPG6).

Key achievements of the proposed VNMM-MLPG include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Closed-Form Exact Integration:} Derived analytical closed-form formulas for local stiffness and mass matrices, totally eliminating numerical quadrature error.
    \item \textbf{Machine-Precision Consistency:} Verified linear consistency via the Galerkin patch test across seven affine vector states on regular and 25\% perturbed point clouds, achieving interior errors on the order of $\sim 10^{-15}$.
    \item \textbf{Optimal MMS Convergence:} Established monotonic asymptotic convergence with optimal rates of $\mathcal{O}(h^{2.06})$ in $L^2(\mathbf{E})$ and $\mathcal{O}(h^{1.24})$ in $H(\mathbf{curl})$.
    \item \textbf{Spectral Purity and Accuracy:} Successfully resolved the 2D PEC resonant cavity problem, reproducing the first 10 transverse electric cutoff wavenumbers with an average error of $4.13\%$ and achieving total suppression of gradient spurious modes for $s_{\mathrm{div}} \ge 10$.
    \item \textbf{Superior Computational Efficiency:} Achieved a $3.59\times$ matrix assembly speedup and a $36\%$ reduction in matrix non-zeros compared to cell-based VNMM-Gauss.
\end{enumerate}

Future work will focus on extending the closed-form spherical formulation to 3D electromagnetic cavities, developing interface-conforming director treatments for multi-dielectric media, and implementing open boundary conditions via Perfectly Matched Layers (PML) in the truly cell-free framework.
