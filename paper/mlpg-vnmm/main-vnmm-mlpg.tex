\abstract[Abstract]{The Vector Nodal Meshless Method (VNMM) approximates $H(\mathbf{curl})$ vector fields on unstructured point clouds using directional nodal degrees of freedom. However, previous implementations relied on background integration cells, introducing intra-cell stencil discontinuities, quadrature overhead, and compromised meshless autonomy. This paper presents a truly cell-free Vector Nodal Meshless Method via Meshless Local Petrov--Galerkin (VNMM-MLPG). Combining linear vector point interpolation with weak forms over autonomous circular subdomains decouples the spatial discretization from background grids. Because fixed linear stencils yield constant curl and divergence per subdomain, Stokes' and Gauss' theorems imply that axisymmetric weight test functions vanishing on the subdomain boundary (MLPG1) produce identically zero stiffness, motivating a Local Galerkin (MLPG6) approach. To eliminate the structural asymmetry and loss of stability observed in tested row-by-row MLPG assemblies without lens-intersection quadratures, we formulate an overlapping \textit{Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin} scheme evaluated in closed form, yielding symmetric positive-semidefinite global operators. Furthermore, modal decomposition shows that single-disk integration leaves two harmonic zero-energy deformation modes---pure shear and hyperbolic---in the local div-curl kernel. We address this rank deficiency by projecting these modes onto directional degrees of freedom and introducing a VMS-inspired orthogonal energy stabilization operator, paired with an empirical resolution-scaled divergence penalty. This stabilization is non-neutral on deviatoric affine strain and decays at rate $\mathcal{O}(h_{\mathrm{char}}^2)$. Benchmarks span static affine patch tests ($k_0 = 0$), manufactured solutions under exact local weak loads, and cavity eigenproblems on regular and tested $25\%$-jitter clouds. They confirm exact linear reproduction under local equilibrium and empirical convergence rates close to the expected approximation orders in the tested MMS problems. Compared to the calibrated cell-based VNMM-Gauss configuration, the calibrated VNMM-MLPG shifts spurious gradient modes outside the tested 10-mode operational spectrum, achieves higher accuracy across most tested resolutions, and improves operator sparsity and assembly speed.}

\keywords{Vector Nodal Meshless Method (VNMM), Meshless Local Petrov--Galerkin (MLPG), Point Interpolation Method (PIM), Truly cell-free methods, Div-curl regularization, Orthogonal energy stabilization, $H(\mathbf{curl})$ vector approximation}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Computational electromagnetics (CEM) has traditionally relied on the finite element method (FEM) with mixed edge elements \cite{nedelec1980mixed, monk2003finite, jin2014finite} to resolve vector boundary-value and eigenvalue problems in $H(\mathbf{curl})$. Edge elements assign tangential field components to elemental edges, enforcing trace continuity while naturally accommodating jump discontinuities across dielectric interfaces and satisfying the exact sequence of the de Rham complex. Consequently, irrotational gradient fields reside in the exact discrete nullspace of the curl operator, $\mathrm{grad} \, W_h \subset \ker(\mathrm{curl}_h)$, preventing the emergence of non-physical spurious eigenmodes \cite{bossavit1998computational, boffi2006finite}. Nevertheless, edge elements are constrained by the necessity of generating and maintaining conforming simplex meshes (triangles in 2D, tetrahedra in 3D). Mesh generation remains the primary computational bottleneck in complex CAD geometries, multi-scale devices, moving boundary problems, and topology optimization routines.

To circumvent mesh generation bottlenecks, meshless and meshfree methods have garnered sustained interest across solid mechanics, fluid dynamics, and wave physics \cite{belytschko2023meshfree, liu2009mesh, fasshauer2007meshfree, atluri2004meshless}. In computational electromagnetics, early meshless adaptations employed scalar formulations for electric scalar potentials, magnetic vector potentials, or out-of-plane electric fields governed by scalar Helmholtz equations \cite{viana1999moving, ho2001moving, parreira2006element, nicomedes2012meshless}. Extending meshless concepts to vector fields governed by Maxwell's curl-curl equations proved substantially more challenging. Early vector adaptations attempted to mimic finite element edge conformity by assigning degrees of freedom to pairs of nodes forming virtual edges, as in the Edge Meshless Method (EMM) \cite{lima2017edge}. While effective at suppressing spurious modes, such schemes reintroduce topological connectivity between node pairs, partially compromising the autonomy of the point cloud. On the other hand, purely nodal collocated schemes with vector degrees of freedom typically induce severe spurious mode contamination and derivative aliasing due to the lack of discrete de Rham complex exactness on arbitrary point clouds \cite{bossavit1990spurious, boffi2006finite}.

The Vector Nodal Meshless Method (VNMM), introduced by Ortiz et al.~\cite{ortiz2021nodal, ortiz2023tese} and Gonçalves et al.~\cite{goncalves2023vector, goncalves2025tese}, provides a node-based vector interpolation strategy. Fundamentally, the VNMM can be recognized as the asymptotic limit of the EMM as the virtual edge length tends to zero: by collapsing the node pair into a single evaluation point $\mathbf{x}_I$, the edge circulation reduces to a local directional projection along a unit director $\vec{t}_I$, restoring point autonomy without pairwise connectivity. From an interpolation standpoint, the VNMM constructs shape functions via local polynomial point collocation on support stencils, conceptually generalizing the Point Interpolation Method (PIM) \cite{liu2001point, liu2005introduction} to vector fields endowed with directional degrees of freedom $e_I = \vec{E}(\mathbf{x}_I) \cdot \vec{t}_I$.

In a recent study \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}, the mathematical foundations of the 2D VNMM were systematically examined on unconstrained irregular point clouds. That study resolved several fundamental questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Incomplete Basis Stagnation:} The classical incomplete solenoidal vector basis ($\mathcal{L}^1$, consisting of 3 terms per stencil) was shown to suffer from a persistent $\mathcal{O}(1)$ error plateau in curl approximation due to derivative aliasing between curl and divergence components (dilatational modes of the field gradient tensor \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}) on irregular clouds. This was resolved by establishing the complete linear vector space $\mathcal{P}_1 \times \mathcal{P}_1$ (requiring a 6-node stencil, denoted $\mathcal{P}^1$), which restored full linear completeness and $\mathcal{O}(h)$ curl convergence.
    \item \textbf{Geometric Singularity Mitigation:} A scale-invariant determinant qualification criterion on a dimensionless placement matrix $\widehat{\mathbf{A}}$, coupled with adaptive $k$-d tree searches, was developed to automatically reject nearly singular node and director configurations without empirical parameter tuning.
    \item \textbf{Variational Div-Curl Stabilization:} Because dynamic support stencils across point clouds lack global trace conformity, discrete gradients leak into the positive curl spectrum. A regularized Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin formulation with a volumetric div-curl penalty ($s_{\mathrm{div}}$) was introduced, successfully shifting spurious modes to higher frequencies while preserving physical cavity resonances.
\end{enumerate}

Despite these substantial advances, the formulation reported in \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026} left open a fundamental deficiency: \textbf{the reliance on a Cartesian background integration grid}. As classified by Belytschko et al.~\cite{belytschko1994element} and Atluri and Shen \cite{atluri2002meshless}, methods like the Element-Free Galerkin (EFG) are not \textit{truly cell-free} because evaluation of the global weak form requires an auxiliary background mesh of quadrature cells covering the domain $\Omega$. In the VNMM context, this background scaffolding creates acute numerical challenges:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Intra-Cell Stencil Discontinuities:} In cell-based integration, the 6-node support stencil is selected point-by-point at each Gauss integration point $P_g$. Because nearest-neighbor stencils switch abruptly within the cell interior, the resulting shape functions and derivatives are piecewise discontinuous across intra-cell boundaries. As demonstrated in \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}, under-integrating these cells (e.g., using $2 \times 2$ Gauss points) produces artificial numerical softening that conceals rank deficiencies in the discrete curl operator. Suppressing this artifact required a dense, resolution-matched quadrature ($3 \times 3$ Gauss points per cell, exceeding 3,600 quadrature evaluations for a 441-node grid).
    \item \textbf{Loss of True Meshless Autonomy:} Background integration grids do not demand the strict shape-regularity or aspect-ratio metrics of finite element meshes. However, constructing background cells and trimming cut cells along complex, curved, or 3D boundaries still introduces substantial geometric pre-processing overhead and quadrature complexity, compromising meshless autonomy.
\end{itemize}

Several alternative domain integration strategies have been investigated in the broader meshfree literature \cite{hillman2018performance}, but each presents specific drawbacks when applied to unstructured vector problems:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Direct Nodal Integration (DNI) and Rank Instabilities:} The simplest meshless strategy evaluates the global weak form directly at the nodes. However, as demonstrated by Babu{\v{s}}ka et al.~\cite{babuska2008quadrature} and Hillman and Chen~\cite{hillman2018performance}, DNI underestimates the stored electromagnetic energy of low-order modes, leading to rank deficiencies (sawtooth zero-energy modes) and degraded convergence rates without extensive ad-hoc stabilization.
    \item \textbf{Conforming vs. Non-Conforming Nodal Smoothing (SCNI and SNNI):} Stabilized Conforming Nodal Integration (SCNI) \cite{chen2001stabilized} restores stability and patch test exactness by computing smoothed gradients via boundary integrals over conforming Voronoi dual cells, preserving matrix symmetry \cite{hillman2018performance}. However, constructing Voronoi diagrams on irregular point clouds is geometrically brittle, sensitive to boundary slivers, and computationally demanding in 3D. While Stabilized Non-Conforming Nodal Integration (SNNI) dispenses with Voronoi meshing by using non-conforming local boxes \cite{hillman2018performance}, the loss of partition conformity fails the patch test, necessitating Petrov--Galerkin variational consistency (VC) corrections \cite{chen2013arbitrary, hillman2018performance} that require auxiliary local equation solves.
    \item \textbf{Strong-Form Nodal Collocation:} Enforcing the governing PDE directly at the nodes avoids numerical quadrature \cite{hillman2018performance}. However, for second-order PDEs, collocation requires computing high-order derivatives of shape functions and demands at least quadratic polynomial completeness for convergence \cite{hillman2018performance}. More fundamentally, for vector electromagnetic problems governed by Maxwell's curl-curl equations, strong-form collocation lacks a discrete de Rham complex structure, triggering spectral corruption and unphysical gradient spurious modes \cite{bossavit1990spurious, boffi2006finite}.
    \item \textbf{Discontinuous Galerkin with Trace Jump Penalties:} One could view shifting stencils as non-conforming elements and introduce interface integrals penalizing tangential trace jumps, as in interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (IPDG) formulations for electromagnetics \cite{houston2004mixed, hesthaven2004high}. However, because point-interpolation stencils lack well-defined geometric element boundaries or compact influence domains, identifying and tracking the complex internal discontinuity interfaces across an unstructured point cloud is geometrically ambiguous and computationally intractable.
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Local Weak Forms (MLPG and LPIM):} Formulations based on local subdomains, such as the Meshless Local Petrov--Galerkin (MLPG) method of Atluri and Zhu \cite{atluri1998new, atluri2002meshless} and the Local Point Interpolation Method (LPIM) of Liu and Gu \cite{liu2001local, liu2005introduction}, circumvent background cells entirely by establishing weak forms over autonomous circular disks or spheres $\Omega_s^I$ centered at each node.
\end{itemize}

While MLPG and LPIM have been widely applied to scalar mechanics, acoustics, and electromagnetics \cite{sladek2001local, atluri2002meshless, liu2001local,nicomedes2012meshless}, their extension to vector electromagnetic formulations in $H(\mathbf{curl})$ presents specific theoretical and algebraic challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How should vector test functions $\vec{W}_I$ be chosen on $\Omega_s^I$? In MLS-based MLPG1 \cite{atluri1998new, atluri2002meshless} and radial-basis LRPIM \cite{liu2005introduction}, localized weight functions vanishing on the subdomain boundary ($w(r_s) = 0$) are commonly used because the trial derivatives vary spatially across $\Omega_s^I$. For a fixed polynomial stencil ($\mathcal{P}^1$) \cite{liu2001local, stable_vnmm2d_2026}, however, the curl and divergence are spatially constant on $\Omega_s^I$, so test functions vanishing on $\partial\Omega_s^I$ cause the local stiffness bilinear forms to vanish identically by Stokes' and Gauss' theorems.
    \item How can global matrix symmetry and positive semidefiniteness be attained without lens-intersection quadratures? In the literature on MLPG \cite{atluri2002meshless, nicomedes2012meshless} and LPIM \cite{liu2001local, liu2005introduction}, equations are assembled in a row-by-row Petrov--Galerkin manner (one test function per node). Because the integration domains differ across nodes ($\Omega_s^I \ne \Omega_s^J$), the resulting global stiffness matrix is intrinsically unsymmetric ($K_{Ij} \ne K_{Ji}$). For conservative electromagnetic cavities, this loss of discrete self-adjointness can induce unphysical complex eigenvalues \cite{saad2011numerical, boffi2013mixed} and causes numerical instability under refinement for general non-polynomial fields. Conversely, symmetric overlapping-subdomain approaches such as the Method of Finite Spheres \cite{de2000method, nicomedes2021acoustic} evaluate coupling integrals over overlapping regions $\Omega_s^I \cap \Omega_s^J$. Depending on the variant, this is done via numerical quadrature over individual subdomains (ignoring points outside the field support) \cite{nicomedes2021acoustic}, via specialized cubature over lens-shaped intersections \cite{de2000method}, or by using axis-aligned brick subdomains to simplify intersections \cite{nicomedes2017meshfree}. In contrast, the formulation developed herein bypasses all numerical quadratures and pairwise support-intersection evaluations entirely.
    \item How should boundary conditions and intersections between local subdomains and physical domain boundaries be treated? When a local disk intersects the physical boundary $\partial\Omega$, the integration domain is truncated into a circular segment $\Omega_s^B = \Omega_s \cap \Omega$, posing the challenge of evaluating spatial integrals on truncated geometries in closed form while leveraging the directional Kronecker-delta property inherited from VNMM \cite{ortiz2021nodal, goncalves2023vector, stable_vnmm2d_2026} to avoid singular boundary penalty integrals.
    \item How can discrete mode softening and local operator rank deficiency be resolved? Evaluating linear vector fields on a single circular disk subdomain produces spatially constant curl and divergence, causing the local stiffness contributions $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I$ and $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{div}}^I$ to each possess rank 1. Consequently, their sum $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I + s_{\mathrm{div}}\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{div}}^I$ has rank 2 over the four-dimensional translation-free linear deformation subspace (whereas the local mass matrix $\mathbf{M}^I$ has full rank 6), leaving two harmonic zero-energy linear deformation modes (pure shear and hyperbolic) unpenalized within each stencil. Unlike cell-based $3\times 3$ Gauss quadrature (which couples adjacent stencils within each cell), single-disk integration allows these modes to soften, causing error stagnation and sublinear convergence unless properly stabilized.
\end{enumerate}

In this paper, we address these challenges by formulating, implementing, and validating the \textbf{VNMM-MLPG}---a truly cell-free vector nodal meshless method for 2D electromagnetics. By combining the directional $\mathcal{P}^1$ Vector Nodal Meshless Method \cite{ortiz2021nodal, stable_vnmm2d_2026} with an overlapping \textbf{Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin Assembly} within the Local Galerkin (MLPG6) framework \cite{atluri2002meshless}, the main contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Closed-Form Analytical Discretization of VNMM Operators:} All local curl, divergence, and mass matrices are factored in exact closed form using the directional inverse collocation matrix $\boldsymbol{\beta}^I = \mathbf{A}_I^{-1}$ and analytical geometric moments over interior disks and boundary-truncated circular segments. This eliminates numerical quadrature errors and avoids lens-intersection cubatures.
    \item \textbf{Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin Assembly and Algebraic Operator Structure:} An additive overlapping-subdomain Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin energy assembly yields symmetric positive-semidefinite global operators $\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{M}$ and characterizes the 2D rigid translation kernel. It ensures strict positive-definiteness on fixed meshes after PEC boundary reduction under edge-connected stencil conditions, while avoiding the refinement-related instability observed in tested unsymmetric row-by-row MLPG/LPIM configurations.
    \item \textbf{Modal Rank Decomposition and VMS-Inspired Orthogonal Energy Stabilization:} Analytical projection maps the two harmonic zero-energy deformation modes ($\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{shear}}^I, \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{hyp}}^I$) onto the VNMM directional degrees of freedom. Pairing a VMS-inspired orthogonal energy stabilization operator ($\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}} = c_{\mathrm{stab}}(h_{\mathrm{char}}/L)^2$) with superlinear penalty scaling ($s_{\mathrm{div}} \propto h_{\mathrm{char}}^{-1.5}$) restores full rank 4 over the local linear deformation subspace. This mitigates local mode softening in the tested configurations and yields empirical convergence rates of $p = 2.15\text{--}2.39$ on regular grids and $p = 1.62$ under $25\%$ nodal jitter.
    \item \textbf{Unified Boundary Treatment on Truncated Subdomains:} Combining VNMM directional Kronecker-delta collocation \cite{ortiz2021nodal, stable_vnmm2d_2026} with truncated-disk MLPG6 weak forms eliminates essential Dirichlet rows and columns without Nitsche terms or Lagrange multipliers. Inhomogeneous Neumann and Robin conditions reduce directly to simple 1D boundary segment integrals.
    \item \textbf{Accuracy, Sparsity, and Assembly Speedup over Cell-Based Quadrature:} The formulation is verified across static patch tests, manufactured solutions (achieving empirical rates close to the expected quadratic $L^2$ and linear broken $H(\mathbf{curl})$ semi-norm orders), and subdomain containment sweeps (confirming the classical scaling $\alpha_s = 0.80$ \cite{atluri2002meshless, liu2009mesh} within the stencil bound $\alpha_s \le 1.15$). In PEC cavity eigenproblems, the calibrated cell-free method outperforms the calibrated cell-based VNMM-Gauss in assembly time ($2.0\times\text{--}3.7\times$ faster), matrix sparsity ($20\%\text{--}24\%$ sparser), and spectral accuracy across most tested resolutions.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section~\ref{sec:formulation} details the mathematical formulation of VNMM-MLPG, proves the breakdown of classical weight test functions, derives the closed-form Local Galerkin (MLPG6) matrices, contrasts row-by-row assembly against the symmetric Subdomain Galerkin assembly, and develops the Dimensionless Orthogonal Energy Stabilization for local rank restoration. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the validation roadmap and numerical results across six comprehensive stages: closed-form elemental verification, patch test satisfaction, MMS asymptotic convergence rates, subdomain scaling bounds for $\alpha_s$, resonant cavity spectra with stabilization, and computational benchmarking. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses advantages, current limitations, and open challenges. Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes the paper.

\section{Theoretical Formulation of the VNMM-MLPG}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Continuous Boundary-Value Problem}
\label{subsec:continuous_bvp}

Consider a bounded two-dimensional domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ with a Lipschitz boundary $\partial\Omega$, occupied by a linear, isotropic, non-dispersive medium characterized by relative permeability $\mu_r$ and permittivity $\epsilon_r$. The time-harmonic electric field $\vec{E}(x, y) = [E_x, E_y]^T$ satisfies Maxwell's curl-curl equation. To shift gradient-type spurious modes outside the operational frequency band on non-conforming meshless spaces, we adopt the div-curl regularized vector Helmholtz equation \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla \times \left(\frac{1}{\mu_r} \nabla \times \vec{E}\right) - \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mu_r} \nabla(\nabla \cdot \vec{E}) - k_0^2 \epsilon_r \vec{E} = \vec{J}_s \quad \text{in } \Omega,
    \label{eq:helmholtz_pde}
\end{equation}
where $k_0 = \omega\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}$ is the free-space wavenumber, $s_{\mathrm{div}} \ge 0$ is the dimensionless divergence regularization parameter, and $\vec{J}_s$ is an applied electric current density. In two dimensions with in-plane vector fields $\vec{E} = [E_x, E_y]^T$, the differential operators are defined by:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla \times \vec{E} = \left(\frac{\partial E_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial E_x}{\partial y}\right) \hat{\mathbf{z}}, \qquad \nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \frac{\partial E_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial y}.
\end{equation}

To establish a well-posed boundary-value problem, the physical boundary is partitioned into pairwise disjoint segments $\partial\Omega = \Gamma_D \cup \Gamma_N \cup \Gamma_R$ (with $\Gamma_D \cap \Gamma_N = \Gamma_D \cap \Gamma_R = \Gamma_N \cap \Gamma_R = \emptyset$), where $\vec{n}$ and $\vec{t}$ denote the outward unit normal and unit tangent to $\partial\Omega$, respectively:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dirichlet Boundary Conditions ($\Gamma_D$):} The tangential trace of the electric field is prescribed:
          \begin{equation}
              \vec{n} \times \vec{E} = \vec{g}_D \quad \iff \quad \vec{E} \cdot \vec{t} = g_D \quad \text{on } \Gamma_D.
              \label{eq:pec_bc}
          \end{equation}
          When $g_D = 0$, this represents the homogeneous Dirichlet condition on perfectly conducting (PEC) electric walls ($\Gamma_{\mathrm{PEC}}$). When $g_D \ne 0$, it models inhomogeneous Dirichlet excitations, such as prescribed incident wave profiles or driven waveguide apertures.
    \item \textbf{Neumann Boundary Conditions ($\Gamma_N$):} The natural boundary flux (tangential magnetic field excitation) is specified:
          \begin{equation}
              -\frac{1}{\mu_r} \left(\vec{n} \times (\nabla \times \vec{E})\right) = \vec{g}_N \quad \text{on } \Gamma_N.
              \label{eq:neumann_bvp_pde}
          \end{equation}
          When $\vec{g}_N = \mathbf{0}$, this represents the homogeneous Neumann condition on perfect magnetic conductors (PMC) or electromagnetic symmetry planes. When $\vec{g}_N \ne \mathbf{0}$, under the $e^{j\omega t}$ time-harmonic convention where $\nabla \times \vec{E} = -j\omega\mu_0\mu_r\vec{H}$, it specifies the impressed tangential magnetic flux $\vec{g}_N = j\omega\mu_0 (\vec{n} \times \vec{H})$.
    \item \textbf{Robin / Absorbing Boundary Conditions ($\Gamma_R$):} To truncate open computational domains or model surface impedance, first-order absorbing boundary conditions (ABC) of the Silver--M{\"u}ller type are imposed:
          \begin{equation}
              -\frac{1}{\mu_r} \left(\vec{n} \times (\nabla \times \vec{E})\right) + \gamma \vec{E}_t = \vec{g}_R \quad \text{on } \Gamma_R,
              \label{eq:robin_bvp_pde}
          \end{equation}
          where $\gamma = j k_0 \sqrt{\epsilon_r / \mu_r}$ is the surface admittance, $\vec{E}_t = (\vec{E} \cdot \vec{t})\vec{t}$ is the tangential electric field vector along the boundary unit tangent $\vec{t}$, and $\vec{g}_R$ represents incoming plane or guided waves.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Nodal Discretization and Local Support Geometries}
\label{subsec:nodal_discretization}

The domain $\overline{\Omega} = \Omega \cup \partial\Omega$ is discretized by a cloud of $N$ nodes $\mathcal{X}_N = \{P_I\}_{I=1}^N$ with Cartesian coordinates $\mathbf{x}_I = [x_I, y_I]^T \in \overline{\Omega}$. Each node $P_I$ is endowed with an assigned unit director vector $\vec{t}_I = [t_{x,I}, t_{y,I}]^T \in \mathbb{S}^1$, defining a scalar degree of freedom (DoF):
\begin{equation}
    e_I = \vec{E}(\mathbf{x}_I) \cdot \vec{t}_I.
\end{equation}
Along physical boundaries with prescribed tangential electric fields, directors are oriented strictly tangent to the boundary ($\vec{t}_I \parallel \partial\Omega$). On perfectly conducting walls ($\mathbf{x}_I \in \Gamma_{\mathrm{PEC}}$), condition \eqref{eq:pec_bc} then translates directly into the homogeneous constraint $e_I = 0$, whereas on inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundaries ($\Gamma_D$), it permits direct collocation of the prescribed tangential profile ($e_I = g_D(\mathbf{x}_I)$). Likewise, along material interfaces between distinct media, aligning directors parallel to the interface natively preserves the continuity of the tangential electric field while naturally accommodating physical jump discontinuities in the normal component.

In the MLPG framework, two distinct local domains are defined for each node $P_I$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic_mlpg}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Trial Support Domain ($\Omega_u^I$):} The spatial neighborhood containing an admissible stencil of $K = 6$ nodes used to construct the local trial vector field $\vec{E}_{I,h}(\mathbf{x})$.
    \item \textbf{Local Integration Subdomain ($\Omega_s^I$):} A circular disk of radius $r_s^I$ centered at $\mathbf{x}_I$:
          \begin{equation}
              \Omega_s^I = \left\{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^2 : \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_I\| \le r_s^I \right\}, \qquad r_s^I = \alpha_s h_I,
              \label{eq:subdomain_disk}
          \end{equation}
          where $h_I \equiv \min_{J \ne I} \|\mathbf{x}_J - \mathbf{x}_I\|_2$ denotes the local nearest-neighbor spacing (matching $h_{\mathrm{local}}$ in \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}), and $\alpha_s > 0$ is a dimensionless radius scaling factor. For a rectangular domain of dimensions $L_x \times L_y$ (reference length $L = \max(L_x, L_y)$) discretized on an $N_x \times N_y$ grid, we distinguish three spacing metrics: local spacing $h_I$, nominal reference spacing $h_{\mathrm{nom}} = L/(N_x - 1)$, and domain-averaged spacing $\overline{h} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{I=1}^N h_I$ on perturbed clouds. These are unified into a single characteristic spacing $h_{\mathrm{char}}$, equal to $h_{\mathrm{nom}}$ on regular grids and $\overline{h}$ on disordered clouds. We adopt the baseline value $\alpha_s = 0.80$. As shown in Section~\ref{subsec:stage4_alpha_s}, this choice gives full ($100\%$) domain coverage on regular lattices and $94.4\%$ coverage under $25\%$ nodal jitter without violating trial stencil containment.
\end{enumerate}

A representative nodal distribution with $25\%$ jitter is depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic_mlpg}, where interior directors $\vec{t}_k$ are randomly oriented and boundary directors satisfy $\vec{t}_k \parallel \partial\Omega$, alongside the 6-node trial stencils $\Omega_u$ (Section~\ref{subsec:stencil_selection}). Depending on node position relative to $\partial\Omega$, three subdomain configurations arise:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Case 1 (Fully Interior, $\Omega_s^{I_1} \subset \Omega$):} When $\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x}_{I_1}, \partial\Omega) \ge r_s^{I_1}$, the integration domain is a full circular disk.
    \item \textbf{Case 2 (Truncated Interior, $\Omega_s^{I_2} \cap \Omega$):} When $\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x}_{I_2}, \partial\Omega) < r_s^{I_2}$, the disk crosses $\partial\Omega$ and is truncated to a circular segment bounded by an internal arc $L_{sI_2} = \partial\Omega_s^{I_2} \cap \Omega$ and a straight boundary segment $\Gamma_s^{I_2} = \Omega_s^{I_2} \cap \partial\Omega$.
    \item \textbf{Case 3 (Truncated Boundary, $\Omega_s^B \cap \Omega$):} For $\mathbf{x}_B \in \partial\Omega$, the disk is truncated into a half-disk (or corner sector) bounded by the internal arc $L_{sB} = \partial\Omega_s^B \cap \Omega$ and the boundary segment $\Gamma_s^B = \Omega_s^B \cap \partial\Omega$ where boundary conditions are enforced.
\end{itemize}
As derived in Section~\ref{subsubsec:truncated_subdomains_analytic}, the constant derivatives of $\mathcal{P}^1$ shape functions allow exact closed-form integration over all three cases without background quadrature cells.

\subsection{VNMM Vector Shape Functions and Field Interpolation via $\mathcal{P}^1$}
\label{subsec:trial_space}

Following \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}, within the local evaluation domain of node $P_I$, the vector field $\vec{E}(\mathbf{x})$ is approximated in the complete linear polynomial vector space $\mathcal{P}^1 = \mathcal{P}_1 \times \mathcal{P}_1$. This space is spanned by the basis matrix $\mathbf{P}(\Delta\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 6}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{P}(\Delta\mathbf{x}) = \begin{bmatrix}
        1 & 0 & \Delta x & \Delta y & 0        & 0        \\
        0 & 1 & 0        & 0        & \Delta x & \Delta y
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eq:P_basis_matrix}
\end{equation}
with local coordinates $\Delta\mathbf{x} = [\Delta x, \Delta y]^T = [x - x_I, y - y_I]^T$ centered at node $P_I$.
Each local vector shape function $\vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x})$ associated with the $a$-th node of the support stencil $\Omega_u^I$ ($a = 1, \dots, 6$) is expressed directly as a linear combination of these basis terms:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_I) \boldsymbol{\beta}_a^I,
    \label{eq:P1_shape_function_expansion_mlpg}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\beta}_a^I = [\beta_{1a}^I, \dots, \beta_{6a}^I]^T \in \mathbb{R}^6$ collects the polynomial coefficients for shape function $a$. Enforcing the directional Kronecker-delta projection property \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}_k) \cdot \vec{t}_k = \delta_{ak}, \qquad k = 1, \dots, 6,
    \label{eq:kronecker_projection}
\end{equation}
across all 6 stencil nodes yields the local linear placement system $\mathbf{A}_I \boldsymbol{\beta}_a^I = \boldsymbol{\delta}_a$, where $\boldsymbol{\delta}_a = [\delta_{1a}, \dots, \delta_{6a}]^T$ is the $a$-th canonical basis vector of $\mathbb{R}^6$, $(\Delta x_k, \Delta y_k) \equiv (x_k - x_I, y_k - y_I)$, and the $k$-th row of the moment placement matrix $\mathbf{A}_I \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    (\mathbf{A}_I)_{k, :} = \vec{t}_k^T \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{x}_I) = \begin{bmatrix} t_{kx} & t_{ky} & \Delta x_k t_{kx} & \Delta y_k t_{kx} & \Delta x_k t_{ky} & \Delta y_k t_{ky} \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eq:matrix_A_row}
\end{equation}
Once an admissible stencil $\Omega_u^I$ with non-singular $\mathbf{A}_I$ is selected (Section~\ref{subsec:stencil_selection}), grouping all six systems yields:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}_I \boldsymbol{\beta}^I = \mathbf{I}_{6 \times 6} \implies \boldsymbol{\beta}^I = \mathbf{A}_I^{-1},
    \label{eq:beta_matrix_inversion}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\beta}^I = [\boldsymbol{\beta}_1^I, \dots, \boldsymbol{\beta}_6^I] \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$. Gathering the local stencil DoFs into $\mathbf{e}_I \equiv \mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e} = [e_1, \dots, e_6]^T \in \mathbb{R}^6$ via the Boolean restriction matrix $\mathbf{L}_I \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times N}$ (where $\mathbf{e} = [e_1, \dots, e_N]^T \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is the global DoF vector), the local interpolated electric field is:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{E}_{I,h}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{a=1}^6 \vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) (\mathbf{e}_I)_a = \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_I) \boldsymbol{\beta}^I \mathbf{e}_I.
    \label{eq:field_interpolation_mlpg}
\end{equation}
Because $\vec{N}_a^I$ are linear in $(x, y)$, their curl and divergence are spatially constant across $\Omega_s^I$:
\begin{align}
    (\nabla \times \vec{N}_a^I)_z & = \frac{\partial N_{ay}^I}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial N_{ax}^I}{\partial y} = \beta_{5a}^I - \beta_{4a}^I \equiv c_{\mathrm{curl}, a}^I \in \mathbb{R}, \label{eq:curl_constant} \\
    \nabla \cdot \vec{N}_a^I      & = \frac{\partial N_{ax}^I}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial N_{ay}^I}{\partial y} = \beta_{3a}^I + \beta_{6a}^I \equiv c_{\mathrm{div}, a}^I \in \mathbb{R}. \label{eq:div_constant}
\end{align}
Defining the constant operator vectors $\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I = (\boldsymbol{\beta}_{5, :}^I - \boldsymbol{\beta}_{4, :}^I)^T \in \mathbb{R}^6$ and $\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I = (\boldsymbol{\beta}_{3, :}^I + \boldsymbol{\beta}_{6, :}^I)^T \in \mathbb{R}^6$, the local curl and divergence evaluate directly as:
\begin{align}
    (\nabla \times \vec{E}_{I,h})_z(\mathbf{x}) & = \sum_{a=1}^6 c_{\mathrm{curl}, a}^I (\mathbf{e}_I)_a = (\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I)^T \mathbf{e}_I \in \mathbb{R}, \label{eq:field_curl_constant} \\
    \nabla \cdot \vec{E}_{I,h}(\mathbf{x})      & = \sum_{a=1}^6 c_{\mathrm{div}, a}^I (\mathbf{e}_I)_a = (\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I)^T \mathbf{e}_I \in \mathbb{R}. \label{eq:field_div_constant}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Formal Definition of the Global Discrete Approximation Space}
\label{subsubsec:discrete_space_definition}

On each active subdomain $D_I \equiv \Omega_s^I \cap \Omega$, the local polynomial reconstruction operator $\Pi_I: \mathbb{R}^N \to \mathcal{P}^1(D_I)$ is defined by:
\begin{equation}
    \Pi_I(\mathbf{e})(\mathbf{x}) \equiv \vec{E}_{I,h}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{a=1}^6 \vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) (\mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e})_a = \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_I) \boldsymbol{\beta}^I \mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e}, \qquad \forall \mathbf{x} \in D_I.
    \label{eq:local_reconstruction_op}
\end{equation}
Because adjacent subdomains overlap ($\Omega_s^I \cap \Omega_s^J \ne \emptyset$) and stencils $\Omega_u^I \ne \Omega_u^J$ are selected autonomously, $\vec{E}_{I,h}$ and $\vec{E}_{J,h}$ do not coincide pointwise on overlaps. Thus, the global approximation space $\mathcal{V}_h$ is a non-conforming subspace of the \emph{broken polynomial product space} $\prod_{I=1}^N \mathcal{P}^1(D_I)$ ($\mathcal{V}_h \not\subset H(\mathbf{curl}, \Omega)$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{V}_h \equiv \left\{ \vec{v}_h = (\vec{v}_{1,h}, \dots, \vec{v}_{N,h}) \in \prod_{I=1}^N \mathcal{P}^1(D_I) \;:\; \exists\, \mathbf{e} \in \mathbb{R}^N \text{ such that } \vec{v}_{I,h} = \Pi_I(\mathbf{e}) \quad \forall I \in \{1, \dots, N\} \right\}.
    \label{eq:global_discrete_space_def}
\end{equation}
Kinematically, the VNMM acts as a directional Vector Point Interpolation Method (Vector PIM) generalizing scalar PIM \cite{liu2001point, liu2005introduction}: unlike MLS or RBF approximations that lack the Kronecker-delta property \cite{atluri2002meshless}, property \eqref{eq:kronecker_projection} enables direct nodal collocation of tangential Dirichlet and PEC boundary conditions without Lagrange multipliers or boundary penalties.

\subsection{Scale-Invariant Stencil Selection Algorithm}
\label{subsec:stencil_selection}

On unstructured or perturbed point clouds, the local moment matrix $\mathbf{A}_I \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$ defined in \eqref{eq:matrix_A_row} can suffer from geometric or directional collinearity singularities ($\det(\mathbf{A}_I) = 0$). To ensure that every node $P_I$ is assigned an algebraically well-conditioned trial support stencil $\Omega_u^I$, we adopt the scale-invariant selection framework established in \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}.
Because columns 1--2 of $\mathbf{A}_I$ are dimensionless ($\mathcal{O}(1)$) while columns 3--6 scale with physical distances ($\mathcal{O}(h_I)$), the determinant exhibits a physical quartic scaling: $\det(\mathbf{A}_I) \sim \mathcal{O}(h_I^4)$. Normalizing local coordinates by $h_I$ yields the scale-invariant admissibility criterion:
\begin{equation}
    |\det(\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_I)| = \frac{|\det(\mathbf{A}_I)|}{h_I^4} \ge \widehat{\mathrm{Tol}} = 0.80,
    \label{eq:scale_invariant_det_criterion}
\end{equation}
which bounds the spectral 2-norm condition number of $\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_I$ to $\kappa_2(\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_I) \le 5.9$ and preserves compact nearest-neighbor supports ($K_{\mathrm{avg}} \approx 6.5\text{--}7.0$) without threshold decay under refinement \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}. Candidate stencils are assembled via a $k$-d tree query around $\mathbf{x}_I$, anchoring $\mathbf{x}_{(1)} = \mathbf{x}_I$ and selecting the nearest 5-combination satisfying \eqref{eq:scale_invariant_det_criterion}. Crucially, in VNMM-MLPG, stencil selection is executed \textbf{strictly once per node} ($N$ evaluations in total), avoiding the $\approx 10 N_{\mathrm{DoF}}$ Gauss-point queries required in cell-based VNMM-Gauss \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}.

\subsection{Local Weak Form and Overlapping Subdomain Energy Formulation}
\label{subsec:local_weak_form}

Let the union of active integration subdomains define the \emph{covered domain region}:
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{\mathrm{cov}} \equiv \bigcup_{I=1}^N D_I \subseteq \Omega.
    \label{eq:covered_region_def}
\end{equation}
When $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}} = \Omega$ (as holds on regular lattices with $\alpha_s \ge \sqrt{2}/2$), the subdomains form a complete overlapping cover of $\Omega$; whereas on perturbed clouds with fixed $\alpha_s = 0.80$ and nearest-neighbor spacing $h_I$, they form a partial cover ($\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}} \subset \Omega$, covering $94.4\%$ of $\Omega$ under $25\%$ jitter, Section~\ref{subsec:stage4_alpha_s}) unless an adaptive radius is adopted. Taking the inner product of the regularized Helmholtz equation \eqref{eq:helmholtz_pde} with a local test function $\vec{W}_I(\mathbf{x})$ over $D_I$ and applying Green's first vector identity yields the local weak identity:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \iint_{D_I} \bigg[ \frac{1}{\mu_r} (\nabla \times \vec{W}_I)_z (\nabla \times \vec{E})_z & + \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mu_r} (\nabla \cdot \vec{W}_I) (\nabla \cdot \vec{E}) - k_0^2 \epsilon_r \vec{W}_I \cdot \vec{E} \bigg] d\Omega \\
                                                                                                    & = \iint_{D_I} \vec{W}_I \cdot \vec{J}_s \, d\Omega + \int_{L_{sI}} \vec{W}_I \cdot \vec{\tau}(\vec{E}) \, ds \\
                                                                                                    & \quad + \int_{\Gamma_s^I} \vec{W}_I \cdot \vec{\tau}_{\mathrm{ext}} \, ds,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:local_weak_form}
\end{equation}
where $\partial D_I = L_{sI} \cup \Gamma_s^I$ comprises the internal circular arc $L_{sI} = \partial\Omega_s^I \cap \Omega$ and the physical boundary segment $\Gamma_s^I = \Omega_s^I \cap \partial\Omega$ carrying prescribed natural fluxes $\vec{\tau}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:boundary_treatment}), and $\vec{\tau}(\vec{E}) = -\mu_r^{-1}(\vec{n} \times (\nabla \times \vec{E})) + s_{\mathrm{div}}\mu_r^{-1}(\nabla \cdot \vec{E})\vec{n}$ is the natural boundary flux along the outward normal $\vec{n}$ derived from \eqref{eq:helmholtz_pde}.

As shown in Section~\ref{subsec:mlpg6_selection}, test functions vanishing on $L_{sI}$ (MLPG1 \cite{atluri1998new, atluri2002meshless}) yield identically zero stiffness for $\mathcal{P}^1$ stencils, whereas row-by-row MLPG6/LPIM testing ($\vec{W}_I = \vec{N}_{i_0}^I$ on $\Omega_s^I \ne \Omega_s^J$) \cite{liu2001local, nicomedes2012meshless} produces unsymmetric global matrices. Conversely, symmetric overlapping-subdomain methods such as the Method of Finite Spheres \cite{de2000method, nicomedes2017meshfree, nicomedes2021acoustic} require numerical quadrature over interacting subdomain pairs. To achieve symmetry without curved arc integrals over $L_{sI}$ or pairwise intersection quadratures, we formulate a \textbf{Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin (SSG)} scheme by defining a discrete quadratic action functional $J_h: \mathbb{R}^N \to \mathbb{R}$ accumulated over the active subdomains $\{D_I\}_{I=1}^N$:
\begin{equation}
    J_h(\mathbf{e}) = \sum_{I=1}^N \left\{ \frac{1}{2} a_{D_I}(\vec{E}_{I,h}, \vec{E}_{I,h}) - \frac{1}{2} k_0^2 m_{D_I}(\vec{E}_{I,h}, \vec{E}_{I,h}) - \ell_{D_I}(\vec{E}_{I,h}) \right\} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{e}^T (\mathbf{K} - k_0^2 \mathbf{M}) \mathbf{e} - \mathbf{e}^T \mathbf{F},
    \label{eq:discrete_action_functional}
\end{equation}
where the local symmetric bilinear forms on $D_I$ are defined in volume energy form:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        a_{D_I}(\vec{W}_I, \vec{E}) - k_0^2 m_{D_I}(\vec{W}_I, \vec{E}) & \equiv \iint_{D_I} \bigg[ \frac{1}{\mu_r} (\nabla \times \vec{W}_I)_z (\nabla \times \vec{E})_z \\
                                                                        & \qquad + \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mu_r} (\nabla \cdot \vec{W}_I) (\nabla \cdot \vec{E}) - k_0^2 \epsilon_r \vec{W}_I \cdot \vec{E} \bigg] d\Omega = \ell_{D_I}(\vec{W}_I).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:volume_energy_form}
\end{equation}
For conservative cavity eigenproblems ($\ell_{D_I} \equiv 0$), stationarity of the discrete Rayleigh quotient ($\delta J_h(\mathbf{e}) = \mathbf{0}$) yields the symmetric generalized eigenvalue problem:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K} \mathbf{e} = \lambda_h \mathbf{M} \mathbf{e}, \qquad \lambda_h = \frac{\mathbf{e}^T \mathbf{K} \mathbf{e}}{\mathbf{e}^T \mathbf{M} \mathbf{e}} = \frac{a_h(\vec{E}_h, \vec{E}_h)}{m_h(\vec{E}_h, \vec{E}_h)},
    \label{eq:rayleigh_quotient_overlap}
\end{equation}
with broken bilinear forms $a_h(\vec{v}_h, \vec{w}_h) = \sum_{I=1}^N a_{D_I}(\vec{v}_{I,h}, \vec{w}_{I,h})$ and $m_h(\vec{v}_h, \vec{w}_h) = \sum_{I=1}^N m_{D_I}(\vec{v}_{I,h}, \vec{w}_{I,h})$.

\subsubsection{Local Green Identity, Global Consistency Residual, and Convergence Characterization}
\label{subsubsec:variational_consistency_strang}

For an exact field $\vec{E}$ satisfying the governing differential equation \eqref{eq:helmholtz_pde} in $\Omega$, Green's vector identity \eqref{eq:local_weak_form} on each $D_I$ gives, for any local test function $\vec{W}_{I,h}$:
\begin{equation}
    a_{D_I}(\vec{E}, \vec{W}_{I,h}) - k_0^2 m_{D_I}(\vec{E}, \vec{W}_{I,h}) - \ell_{D_I}^{\mathrm{vol}}(\vec{W}_{I,h}) = \int_{L_{sI}} \vec{\tau}(\vec{E}) \cdot \vec{W}_{I,h} \, ds + \int_{\Gamma_s^I} \vec{\tau}(\vec{E}) \cdot \vec{W}_{I,h} \, ds,
    \label{eq:local_green_flux_balance}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_{D_I}^{\mathrm{vol}}$ contains the prescribed volumetric source contribution. The second integral represents the physical boundary contribution, whereas the first arises from the artificial boundary introduced by the local integration subdomain.

Unlike element-based discretizations, the circular subdomains $\{D_I\}_{I=1}^N$ do not form a disjoint partition of $\Omega$. Their artificial boundaries $L_{sI}$ are generally non-coincident, and the associated local test functions are independently reconstructed from different directional stencils. Consequently, the artificial boundary contributions cannot, in general, be assumed to cancel pairwise.

For the assembled non-conforming formulation, the consistency residual therefore contains the aggregate contribution:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_h(\vec{w}_h) = \sum_{I=1}^N \int_{L_{sI}} \vec{\tau}(\vec{E}) \cdot \vec{w}_{I,h} \, ds,
    \label{eq:global_consistency_residual}
\end{equation}
after accounting for the prescribed physical boundary terms. The magnitude and asymptotic behavior of this residual depend on the interaction between the local test functions, the overlap geometry, and the inter-stencil discrepancies. In particular, a scaled trace inequality alone does not establish a vanishing order for the aggregate flux contribution; an additional estimate controlling these interactions would be required.

Accordingly, the present analysis distinguishes the exact local Green identity and the algebraic properties of the assembled operators from a global asymptotic consistency estimate:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Demonstrated Algebraic and Local Approximation Properties:}
          \begin{itemize}
              \item \emph{Exact Affine Reproduction ($\vec{E}^* \in \mathcal{P}^1$):} Defining the exact nodal DoF vector $\mathbf{e}^* \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with entries $e_k^* = \vec{E}^*(\mathbf{x}_k) \cdot \vec{t}_k$, Green's identity \eqref{eq:local_green_flux_balance} ensures $a_{D_I}(\vec{E}^*, \vec{W}_{I,h}) - k_0^2 m_{D_I}(\vec{E}^*, \vec{W}_{I,h}) = \ell_{D_I}(\vec{W}_{I,h})$ on each subdomain when the local load $\ell_{D_I}$ incorporates the complete subdomain boundary flux. Since $\mathcal{P}^1$ reproduces linear fields exactly ($\vec{E}_{I,h} \equiv \vec{E}^*$), every local residual vanishes to machine precision:
                    \begin{equation}
                        \mathbf{r}_I(\mathbf{e}^*) \equiv (\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}} - k_0^2 \mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}}) \mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e}^* - \mathbf{f}_I^{\mathrm{local}} \equiv \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{e}^*) = \sum_{I=1}^N \mathbf{L}_I^T \mathbf{r}_I(\mathbf{e}^*) \equiv \mathbf{0},
                        \label{eq:subdomain_residual_zero}
                    \end{equation}
                    verifying the local Green identity, polynomial reproduction, and algebraic assembly implementation regardless of overlap multiplicity $M(\mathbf{x})$.
              \item \emph{Local Taylor Approximation and Overlap Jumps:} For smooth fields $\vec{E} \in [H^2(\Omega)]^2$ and well-conditioned stencils ($\kappa_2(\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_I) \le 5.9$), local polynomial interpolation on $D_I$ satisfies standard Taylor bounds:
                    \begin{equation}
                        \|\vec{E} - \vec{E}_{I,h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^2(D_I)} \le C_1 h_I^2 \|\vec{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^2(\Omega_u^I)}, \qquad
                        \|\nabla(\vec{E} - \vec{E}_{I,h})\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^2(D_I)} \le C_2 h_I \|\vec{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^2(\Omega_u^I)},
                        \label{eq:local_taylor_bounds}
                    \end{equation}
                    so inter-stencil discrepancies across any overlap $\Omega_s^{IJ} \equiv \Omega_s^I \cap \Omega_s^J \ne \emptyset$ scale as:
                    \begin{align}
                        \|\vec{E}_{I,h} - \vec{E}_{J,h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^2(\Omega_s^{IJ})} & \le C_3 h_{\mathrm{char}}^2 \|\vec{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^2(\Omega_u^I \cup \Omega_u^J)}, \label{eq:stencil_jump_L2} \\
                        \|\operatorname{curl}\vec{E}_{I,h} - \operatorname{curl}\vec{E}_{J,h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^2(\Omega_s^{IJ})} & \le C_4 h_{\mathrm{char}} \|\vec{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^2(\Omega_u^I \cup \Omega_u^J)}. \label{eq:stencil_jump_curl}
                    \end{align}
              \item \emph{Norm Equivalence on the Covered Region $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}$:} Defining the pointwise overlap multiplicity $M(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{I=1}^N \chi_{D_I}(\mathbf{x})$, where $1 \le M_{\min} \le M(\mathbf{x}) \le M_{\max} < \infty$ almost everywhere in $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}$ (assuming a locally finite cover, with $M_{\max}$ denoting the maximum overlap multiplicity of the discretization family), the subdomain-accumulated $L^2$ form satisfies two-sided equivalence on $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}$:
                    \begin{equation}
                        M_{\min} \|\vec{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^2(\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}})}^2 \le \sum_{I=1}^N \|\vec{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^2(D_I)}^2 = \iint_{\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}} M(\mathbf{x}) |\vec{v}(\mathbf{x})|^2 \, d\Omega \le M_{\max} \|\vec{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^2(\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}})}^2 \quad \forall \vec{v} \in \boldsymbol{L}^2(\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}).
                        \label{eq:norm_equivalence_multiplicity}
                    \end{equation}
                    On configurations with full coverage ($\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}} = \Omega$, such as regular grids where $\bar{M} \approx \pi\alpha_s^2 \approx 2.01$), Eq.~\eqref{eq:norm_equivalence_multiplicity} establishes global norm equivalence on $\boldsymbol{L}^2(\Omega)$; on perturbed clouds with partial coverage ($\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}} \subset \Omega$), it holds strictly over the covered region $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}$.
          \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Conditional Energy Error Structure (Strang's First Lemma):}
          The assembled stiffness and mass matrices retain the symmetry and semidefiniteness properties established in Section~\ref{subsubsec:symmetric_assembly}. In the non-conforming broken setting, the abstract energy error is governed by Strang's First Lemma \cite{ciarlet2002finite, brenner2008mathematical}:
          \begin{equation}
              \|\vec{E} - \vec{E}_h\|_{h, a} \le \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma_a}{\alpha_h} \right) \inf_{\vec{v}_h \in \mathcal{V}_h} \|\vec{E} - \vec{v}_h\|_{h, a} + \frac{1}{\alpha_h} \sup_{\vec{w}_h \in \mathcal{V}_h \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}} \frac{|\mathcal{R}_h(\vec{w}_h)|}{\|\vec{w}_h\|_{h, a}},
              \label{eq:strang_lemma}
          \end{equation}
          where $\gamma_a > 0$ is the continuity constant of $a_h(\cdot, \cdot)$ and $\alpha_h > 0$ is the discrete coercivity constant. However, establishing a rigorous closed-form global bound on $\mathcal{R}_h(\vec{w}_h)$ across the non-paired internal circular arcs $L_{sI}$ together with a mesh-independent stability estimate ($\alpha_h \ge \alpha_0 > 0$ as $h_{\mathrm{char}} \to 0$) over overlapping covers remain open theoretical questions. Furthermore, deriving second-order $L^2$ convergence from energy estimates would additionally require a non-conforming Aubin--Nitsche duality argument. Thus, asymptotic energy convergence via \eqref{eq:strang_lemma} is treated strictly as a conditional theoretical framework.
    \item \textbf{Empirically Observed Convergence Rates:}
          Given these open theoretical questions on general overlapping geometries, asymptotic convergence orders are established numerically rather than derived from a completed global proof. Specifically, rates close to $\mathcal{O}(h_{\mathrm{nom}}^2)$ in the multiplicity-weighted broken $L^2$ field norm and $\mathcal{O}(h_{\mathrm{nom}})$ in the broken rotational semi-norm are observed in the MMS tests of Section~\ref{subsec:stage3_mms}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Selection of the MLPG6 (Local Galerkin) Test Space}
\label{subsec:mlpg6_selection}

A central question in local meshless methods concerns the choice of the vector test space $\vec{W}_I$. In MLS-based MLPG1 \cite{atluri1998new, atluri2002meshless} and radial-basis LRPIM \cite{liu2005introduction}, localized radial weight functions that vanish continuously on the subdomain boundary ($w_I(r_s^I) = 0$) are routinely employed because the MLS/RBF trial derivatives vary spatially within $\Omega_s^I$. In the present vector PIM setting with a fixed linear polynomial stencil ($\mathcal{P}^1$) per subdomain \cite{liu2001local, stable_vnmm2d_2026}, however, using vanishing test functions ($\vec{W}_I|_{\partial\Omega_s^I} = \mathbf{0}$) causes the stiffness integral to vanish identically (analogous to the vanishing of constant-gradient domain integrals for compactly supported weights in scalar LPIM \cite{liu2001local}). Because the curl and divergence of linear vector trial fields ($\mathcal{P}^1$) are spatially constant within $\Omega_s^I$ (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:field_curl_constant}--\eqref{eq:field_div_constant}), Stokes' circulation theorem and Gauss' divergence theorem convert both volume stiffness integrals directly into closed contour integrals of the test function along $\partial\Omega_s^I$. Since $\vec{W}_I$ vanishes on the boundary, both integrals vanish identically, causing the entire stiffness matrix to collapse to zero ($\mathbf{K} \equiv \mathbf{0}$). Consequently, weight-based MLPG1 testing is unsuitable for fixed-stencil first-order vector approximations.

To resolve this limitation and preserve the mathematical structure of the continuous Maxwell operators, we adopt the \textbf{MLPG6 (Local Bubnov--Galerkin)} framework \cite{atluri2002meshless}, choosing the vector test functions directly from the space of \textbf{trial shape functions themselves} within each local subdomain $\Omega_s^I$:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{W}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) = \vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) \quad \forall \mathbf{x} \in \Omega_s^I, \quad a = 1, \dots, 6.
    \label{eq:mlpg6_choice}
\end{equation}
This Local Galerkin choice confers three key advantages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact Symmetry and Positive-Definiteness:} Testing with the trial basis ($\vec{W}_a^I = \vec{N}_a^I$) ensures that the local stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}}$ and mass matrix $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}}$ are formed by inner products of the basis functions with themselves. This ensures that every local matrix is symmetric and positive-(semi)definite, naturally preserving the self-adjointness of the vector curl-curl operator and preventing unphysical complex eigenvalues.
    \item \textbf{Non-Trivial Stiffness Transmission:} Because $(\nabla \times \vec{N}_a^I)_z$ and $\nabla \cdot \vec{N}_a^I$ are non-zero constants, their inner products against themselves are strictly positive, completely resolving the null-stiffness collapse of MLPG1.
    \item \textbf{Exact Closed-Form Integration:} Integrands reduce to low-order Cartesian polynomials, enabling exact closed-form analytical integration over both circular and truncated subdomains without numerical quadrature errors (derived in Section~\ref{subsec:analytic_formulas} and \ref{app:truncated_geometry}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Closed-Form Analytical Discretization Formulas}
\label{subsec:analytic_formulas}

Let $\Omega_s^I$ be a circular disk of radius $r_s^I$. Its area and polar second moment of area are:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        S_0^{\mathrm{full}} & = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} d\Omega = \pi (r_s^I)^2,                                                                \\
        S_2^{\mathrm{full}} & = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (x - x_I)^2 d\Omega = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (y - y_I)^2 d\Omega = \frac{\pi (r_s^I)^4}{4}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:disk_moments}
\end{equation}
All odd moments vanish identically by radial symmetry:
\begin{equation}
    \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (x - x_I) \, d\Omega = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (y - y_I) \, d\Omega = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} (x - x_I)(y - y_I) \, d\Omega \equiv 0.
\end{equation}

Within each subdomain $\Omega_s^I$, the complete local trial basis consists of the 6 vector shape functions $\vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x})$ ($a=1,\dots,6$). Substituting \eqref{eq:P1_shape_function_expansion_mlpg} into the local weak form \eqref{eq:local_weak_form}, the local stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}} \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$ is partitioned into curl and divergence contributions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}} = \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{curl}}^{\mathrm{local}} + s_{\mathrm{div}} \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{div}}^{\mathrm{local}},
\end{equation}
where, using the constant vectors $\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I, \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I \in \mathbb{R}^6$ defined in \eqref{eq:curl_constant}--\eqref{eq:div_constant}:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{curl}}^{\mathrm{local}} & = \frac{1}{\mu_r} \left( \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I (\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I)^T \right) \pi (r_s^I)^2, \label{eq:k_curl_analytic} \\
    \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{div}}^{\mathrm{local}}  & = \frac{1}{\mu_r} \left( \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I (\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I)^T \right) \pi (r_s^I)^2. \label{eq:k_div_analytic}
\end{align}
Both matrices are outer products of vectors with themselves, hence manifestly symmetric and positive-semidefinite.

For the local mass matrix $M_{I, ab}^{\mathrm{local}} = \iint_{\Omega_s^I} \epsilon_r (\vec{N}_a^I \cdot \vec{N}_b^I) \, d\Omega$, expanding the dot product via \eqref{eq:P1_shape_function_expansion_mlpg} with $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1:2, a}^I = [\beta_{1a}^I, \beta_{2a}^I]^T$ and $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{3:6, a}^I = [\beta_{3a}^I, \beta_{4a}^I, \beta_{5a}^I, \beta_{6a}^I]^T$ and integrating over the disk yields the closed-form expression:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}} = \epsilon_r \left( \mathbf{C}_0^I \pi (r_s^I)^2 + \mathbf{C}_1^I \frac{\pi (r_s^I)^4}{4} \right),
    \label{eq:m_local_analytic}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{C}_0^I = (\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1:2}^I)^T \boldsymbol{\beta}_{1:2}^I \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$ and $\mathbf{C}_1^I = (\boldsymbol{\beta}_{3:6}^I)^T \boldsymbol{\beta}_{3:6}^I \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$ are Gram matrices, ensuring that $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}}$ is symmetric and positive-definite.

\subsubsection{Closed-Form Discretization for Truncated Subdomains (Cases 2 and 3)}
\label{subsubsec:truncated_subdomains_analytic}

When a local subdomain $\Omega_s^I$ intersects $\partial\Omega$ (Cases~2 and 3 in Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic_mlpg}), the active integration domain is $D_I \equiv \Omega_s^I \cap \Omega$. Because the curl and divergence of $\mathcal{P}^1$ shape functions are spatially constant (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:field_curl_constant}--\eqref{eq:field_div_constant}), the stiffness integrand factors outside the integral:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{curl}}^{\mathrm{trunc}} = \frac{S_0^I}{\mu_r} \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I (\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I)^T, \qquad
    \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{div}}^{\mathrm{trunc}} = \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}} S_0^I}{\mu_r} \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I (\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I)^T,
    \label{eq:k_curl_truncated}
\end{equation}
where $S_0^I = \iint_{D_I} d\Omega$ denotes the active subdomain area. Defining the dimensionless active area fraction $\eta_I \equiv S_0^I / S_0^{\mathrm{full}} \in (0, 1]$ and denoting the untrimmed full-disk operators from \eqref{eq:k_curl_analytic}--\eqref{eq:m_local_analytic} by $\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{full}}$ and $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{full}}$, \textbf{the exact truncated stiffness matrix is obtained by simple area scaling}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{trunc}} = \eta_I \mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{full}}.
    \label{eq:k_truncated_scaled}
\end{equation}
Unlike classical MLPG strategies that shrink near-boundary radii tangentially ($r_s^I = \min(r_{s,0}^I, \mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x}_I, \partial\Omega))$ \cite{atluri2002meshless}) or map truncated sectors onto reference circles for Gauss quadrature \cite{atluri2004meshless}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:k_truncated_scaled} requires only the scalar area fraction $\eta_I$. Because each disk $\Omega_s^I$ is centered at a physical node $\mathbf{x}_I \in \overline{\Omega}$, on any convex polygonal domain with interior corner angles $\theta_{\mathrm{corner}}$ the area fraction satisfies $\eta_I \ge \theta_{\mathrm{corner}}/(2\pi)$ ($\eta_I \ge 1/2$ on flat walls and $\eta_I \ge 1/4$ at $90^\circ$ corners). This lower bound prevents the small-cut ill-conditioning ($\eta \to 0$) typical of unfitted cell methods such as CutFEM \cite{hansbo2002unfitted}.

For the local mass matrix $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}}$ on a truncated subdomain, broken reflection symmetry introduces first- and second-order geometric moments relative to $\mathbf{x}_I$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{trunc}} = \epsilon_r \left( \mathbf{P}_0^I S_0^I + \mathbf{P}_x^I S_{1x}^I + \mathbf{P}_y^I S_{1y}^I + \mathbf{P}_{xx}^I S_{2xx}^I + \mathbf{P}_{yy}^I S_{2yy}^I + \mathbf{P}_{xy}^I S_{2xy}^I \right),
    \label{eq:m_truncated_moments}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{P}_0^I, \dots, \mathbf{P}_{xy}^I \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$ are constant outer-product matrices of rows of $\boldsymbol{\beta}^I$ from \eqref{eq:P1_shape_function_expansion_mlpg}, and $S_{pq}^I = \iint_{D_I} (x - x_I)^p (y - y_I)^q \, d\Omega$. Closed-form analytical expressions for $\eta_I$ and $S_{pq}^I$ across rectilinear walls and orthogonal corners are derived in \ref{app:truncated_geometry}.

\subsubsection{Classical Row-by-Row MLPG Assembly and Matrix Symmetry Analysis}
\label{subsubsec:row_mlpg_symmetry}

In classical row-by-row MLPG/LPIM \cite{atluri1998new, atluri2002meshless, liu2001local}, each node $P_I$ is tested with a single function $\vec{W}_I(\mathbf{x}) = \vec{N}_{i_0}^I(\mathbf{x})$ (where $\mathbf{x}_{i_0} = \mathbf{x}_I$ in $\Omega_u^I$). Note that if the contour integral $-\int_{L_{sI}} \vec{W}_I \cdot \vec{\tau}(\vec{E}_{I,h})\,ds$ in \eqref{eq:local_weak_form} is evaluated using the same fixed linear stencil $\Omega_u^I$, Green's identity causes the volume and boundary stiffness terms to cancel identically (requiring numerical quadrature across shifting neighbor stencils along $L_{sI}$ in classical LPIM \cite{liu2001local}). Testing the volume form \eqref{eq:volume_energy_form} with $\vec{W}_I = \vec{N}_{i_0}^I$ yields the $I$-th row equation $\sum_{k=1}^6 K_{I, k}^{\mathrm{row}} (\mathbf{e}_I)_k = F_I$ in closed form:
\begin{equation}
    K_{I, k}^{\mathrm{row}} = \left[ \frac{1}{\mu_r} c_{\mathrm{curl}, i_0}^I c_{\mathrm{curl}, k}^I + \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mu_r} c_{\mathrm{div}, i_0}^I c_{\mathrm{div}, k}^I \right] \pi (r_s^I)^2.
    \label{eq:k_row_analytic}
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Inherent Matrix Unsymmetry in Classical MLPG and LPIM]
    The global matrices $\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{M}$ assembled row-by-row via \eqref{eq:k_row_analytic} are intrinsically unsymmetric ($K_{Ij} \ne K_{Ji}$) \cite{atluri2002meshless, liu2001local} due to: (i) distinct integration domains ($\Omega_s^I \ne \Omega_s^J$ for $\mathbf{x}_I \ne \mathbf{x}_J$), and (ii) autonomous support stencils ($\Omega_u^I \ne \Omega_u^J \implies \boldsymbol{\beta}^I \ne \boldsymbol{\beta}^J$). While row-by-row assembly passes the linear patch test because affine fields have zero local residual on every disk, testing with a single function per disk on non-polynomial fields loses self-adjointness and discrete coercivity under refinement (Section~\ref{subsubsec:mms_assembly_comparison}).
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin Assembly and Algebraic Stability Hierarchy}
\label{subsubsec:symmetric_assembly}

To preserve self-adjointness in conservative eigenproblems and obtain symmetric positive-semidefinite discrete operators for source-driven formulations while remaining cell-free, we introduce the \textbf{Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin (SSG)} scheme, which tests each disk $\Omega_s^I$ against \textit{all six} local trial shape functions $\vec{W}_a^I = \vec{N}_a^I$ ($a=1,\dots,6$), accumulating the $6 \times 6$ local matrices \eqref{eq:k_curl_analytic}--\eqref{eq:m_local_analytic} via Boolean scatter operations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K} = \sum_{I=1}^N \mathbf{L}_I^T \mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}} \mathbf{L}_I, \qquad \mathbf{M} = \sum_{I=1}^N \mathbf{L}_I^T \mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}} \mathbf{L}_I, \qquad \mathbf{F} = \sum_{I=1}^N \mathbf{L}_I^T \mathbf{f}_I^{\mathrm{local}},
    \label{eq:global_assembly}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{f}_I^{\mathrm{local}} = [\ell_{D_I}(\vec{N}_1^I), \dots, \ell_{D_I}(\vec{N}_6^I)]^T \in \mathbb{R}^6$. We distinguish four algebraic and spectral properties of \eqref{eq:global_assembly}:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item \textbf{Discrete Symmetry and Real Spectrum ($\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{K}^T$, $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{M}^T$):}
          Because each local block $\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}} \ge 0$ and $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}} > 0$ is a symmetric Gram/outer-product matrix, the congruence sum \eqref{eq:global_assembly} is unconditionally symmetric. Eliminating PEC boundary DoFs preserves symmetry on the reduced pencil $(\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}}, \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{red}})$, guaranteeing strictly real eigenvalues ($\operatorname{Im}(\lambda) = 0$). While symmetry precludes complex-conjugate eigenvalues, real irrotational gradient modes ($\nabla H^1(\Omega)$) are shifted outside the operational band by the divergence penalty $s_{\mathrm{div}}\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{div}}$ ($\lambda_{\mathrm{spur}} \sim \mathcal{O}(s_{\mathrm{div}})$).
    \item \textbf{Global Positive Semidefiniteness of $\mathbf{K}$ and Strict Positive Definiteness of $\mathbf{M}$:}
          For any $\mathbf{e} \in \mathbb{R}^N$, $\mathbf{e}^T \mathbf{K} \mathbf{e} = \sum_{I=1}^N (\mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e})^T \mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}} (\mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e}) \ge 0$. Moreover, because every node $I$ belongs to its own stencil $\Omega_u^I$, any non-zero global vector $\mathbf{e} \ne \mathbf{0}$ yields at least one non-zero local vector $\mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e} \ne \mathbf{0}$. Since $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}} > 0$ on every subdomain of positive area, $\mathbf{e}^T \mathbf{M} \mathbf{e} = \sum_{I=1}^N (\mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e})^T \mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}} (\mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e}) > 0$, proving $\mathbf{M} > 0$ algebraically without relying on global geometric coverage.
    \item \textbf{Positive Definiteness on a Fixed Mesh after PEC Reduction ($\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} > 0$):}
          For $s_{\mathrm{div}} > 0$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}} > 0$, the combined curl--div and orthogonal stabilization operator $\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local, stab}}$ has a local kernel consisting exclusively of constant vector translations ($\vec{E}_{I, h} = \vec{c}_I \in \mathbb{R}^2$), since the curl term penalizes rotation, the divergence term penalizes isotropic dilation, and $\mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{stab}}^{\mathrm{local}}$ penalizes both deviatoric strain modes. Hence $\mathbf{e}^T \mathbf{K} \mathbf{e} = 0$ requires $\mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e} \in \ker(\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local, stab}})$ for all $I$, restricting each local field to $\vec{E}_{I, h} = \vec{c}_I$. Each shared node $k \in \Omega_u^I \cap \Omega_u^J$ imposes the scalar directional projection $e_k = \vec{c}_I \cdot \vec{t}_k = \vec{c}_J \cdot \vec{t}_k$. Under the geometric connectivity condition that the stencil-overlap graph contains a connected spanning subgraph whose edges $(I, J)$ share directional vectors $\{\vec{t}_k : k \in \Omega_u^I \cap \Omega_u^J\}$ spanning $\mathbb{R}^2$, the local translation vectors must coincide across adjacent stencils ($\vec{c}_I = \vec{c}_J$). Consequently, they coincide globally ($\vec{c}_I = \vec{c} \in \mathbb{R}^2$), yielding a two-dimensional translation kernel $\dim(\ker(\mathbf{K})) = 2$. Along PEC boundaries with non-parallel walls, enforcing $\vec{c} \cdot \vec{t}_1 = \vec{c} \cdot \vec{t}_2 = 0$ forces $\vec{c} = \mathbf{0}$, so eliminating PEC DoFs yields:
          \begin{equation}
              \ker(\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}}) = \{ \mathbf{0} \} \implies \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} = \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}}^T > 0 \quad \text{(on a fixed connected mesh)}.
              \label{eq:condensed_positive_definite_proof}
          \end{equation}
          We verified this geometric condition directly across all twelve benchmark point clouds ($15\times 15$ to $49\times 49$ under both $0\%$ and $25\%$ jitter with isotropic random directors, Section~\ref{sec:results}). On every tested cloud, the subgraph formed by full-rank edges connects all $N$ nodes into a single component, with the second largest singular value $\sigma_2$ of the stacked shared-director matrix $[\vec{t}_k^T]_{k \in \Omega_u^I \cap \Omega_u^J}$ satisfying $\sigma_2 \ge 1.0 \times 10^{-3}$.
    \item \textbf{Distinction from Uniform Discrete Coercivity ($\alpha_h \ge \alpha_0 > 0$ as $h_{\mathrm{char}} \to 0$):}
          Finite-dimensional positive-definiteness $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} > 0$ ensures $\alpha(h_{\mathrm{char}}) = \lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}}) > 0$ on each fixed mesh, but does not prove that $\alpha(h_{\mathrm{char}}) \ge \alpha_0 > 0$ uniformly as $h_{\mathrm{char}} \to 0$. Uniform discrete coercivity over overlapping non-conforming spaces remains a theoretical hypothesis, supported empirically by the stable conditioning and convergence observed in Section~\ref{sec:results}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Discrete Operator Rank Deficiency and Orthogonal Energy Stabilization}
\label{subsec:rank_deficiency_stabilization}

\subsubsection{Local Operator Rank Analysis under Linear Vector Basis}
\label{subsubsec:local_rank_analysis}

On each stencil $\Omega_u^I$, the linear field $\vec{E}_{I,h}(\mathbf{x})$ has 6 degrees of freedom $\mathbf{e}_I = \mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e} \in \mathbb{R}^6$ and polynomial coefficients $\mathbf{a} = \boldsymbol{\beta}^I \mathbf{e}_I \in \mathbb{R}^6$. By \eqref{eq:field_curl_constant}--\eqref{eq:field_div_constant} and \eqref{eq:k_curl_analytic}--\eqref{eq:k_div_analytic}, the local curl and divergence stiffness matrices on $D_I$ (active area $S_0^I = \eta_I \pi (r_s^I)^2$) are rank-1 dyadic outer products of $\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I$ and $\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I$. Consequently, the unstabilized local stiffness matrix has rank at most 2:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}}) = \mathrm{rank}\left(\mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{curl}}^{\mathrm{local}} + s_{\mathrm{div}}\mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{div}}^{\mathrm{local}}\right) \le 1 + 1 = 2,
    \label{eq:stiffness_rank_deficiency}
\end{equation}
whereas $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{local}}$ \eqref{eq:m_local_analytic} has full rank 6. Unlike cell-based VNMM-Gauss \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026} (where $3 \times 3$ Gauss points per cell sample shifting neighbor stencils to yield full rank 6), single-disk integration leaves a 4-dimensional local stiffness kernel (two rigid translations plus two harmonic linear deformations), allowing discrete modes to soften and causing sublinear eigenvalue convergence ($p \approx 0.53\text{--}0.84$, Section~\ref{subsubsec:cavity_jitter_error}).

\subsubsection{Canonical Orthogonal Decomposition and VMS-Inspired Stabilization}
\label{subsubsec:canonical_decomposition}

To restore full rank over the 4-dimensional linear deformation subspace, we decompose the 2D gradient tensor $\nabla\vec{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$ into four mutually orthogonal components:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla \vec{E} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2} (\nabla \times \vec{E})_z \mathbf{J}}_{\text{rotational}} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2} (\nabla \cdot \vec{E}) \mathbf{I}_2}_{\text{isotropic dilation}} + \underbrace{\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{E})}_{\text{deviatoric / shear strain}},
    \label{eq:gradient_orthogonal_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{J} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{E}) = \frac{1}{2}(\nabla\vec{E} + (\nabla\vec{E})^T) - \frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot\vec{E})\mathbf{I}_2$. On stencil $\Omega_u^I$, these four gradient modes map to discrete nodal vectors:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{curl}}^I  & = (\boldsymbol{\beta}_{5, :}^I - \boldsymbol{\beta}_{4, :}^I)^T \quad (\text{curl / rotation}), \label{eq:v_curl_def}                   \\
    \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{div}}^I   & = (\boldsymbol{\beta}_{3, :}^I + \boldsymbol{\beta}_{6, :}^I)^T \quad (\text{divergence / trace}), \label{eq:v_div_def}                     \\
    \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{shear}}^I & = (\boldsymbol{\beta}_{5, :}^I + \boldsymbol{\beta}_{4, :}^I)^T \quad (\text{shear strain } 2\varepsilon_{xy}), \label{eq:v_shear_def}                   \\
    \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{hyp}}^I   & = (\boldsymbol{\beta}_{3, :}^I - \boldsymbol{\beta}_{6, :}^I)^T \quad (\text{normal deviatoric strain } \varepsilon_{xx} - \varepsilon_{yy}). \label{eq:v_hyp_def}
\end{align}
Because $\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{W}_I) : \operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{E}_{I,h}) = \frac{1}{2}\left[(\partial_x W_y + \partial_y W_x)(\partial_x E_y + \partial_y E_x) + (\partial_x W_x - \partial_y W_y)(\partial_x E_x - \partial_y E_y)\right]$ and the two traceless deviatoric modes $\{\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{shear}}^I, \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{hyp}}^I\}$ reside in $\ker(\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local}})$, we introduce the \textbf{Orthogonal Energy Stabilization Operator}, corresponding to the local bilinear form $a_{D_I}^{\mathrm{stab}}(\vec{W}_I, \vec{E}) = \iint_{D_I} (2\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}}/\mu_r) \operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{W}_I) : \operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{E}) \, d\Omega = \mathbf{w}_I^T \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{stab}}^{\mathrm{local}} \mathbf{e}_I$ (for local test and trial DoF vectors $\mathbf{w}_I, \mathbf{e}_I \in \mathbb{R}^6$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{stab}}^{\mathrm{local}} = \frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}}}{\mu_r} S_0^I \left[ \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{shear}}^I (\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{shear}}^I)^T + \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{hyp}}^I (\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{hyp}}^I)^T \right], \qquad
    \mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local, stab}} = \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{curl}}^{\mathrm{local}} + s_{\mathrm{div}}\mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{div}}^{\mathrm{local}} + \mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{stab}}^{\mathrm{local}}.
    \label{eq:k_stab_analytic}
\end{equation}
By construction, $\mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{stab}}^{\mathrm{local}}$ vanishes identically on constant translations, pure rotations, and isotropic dilations, while penalizing the two traceless linear strain modes to raise $\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{K}_I^{\mathrm{local, stab}})$ from 2 to 4. Note that because $\mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{stab}}^{\mathrm{local}}$ penalizes deviatoric strain, it is not neutral on shear/hyperbolic affine fields unless balanced in the right-hand functional (Remark~\ref{rem:patch_test_stab}), and it introduces a finite-mesh perturbation in homogeneous eigenvalue problems. Motivated heuristically by Variational Multiscale (VMS) subgrid-scale concepts (rather than a formal multiscale variational splitting), we adopt the quadratic scaling law:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{\mathrm{stab}}(h_{\mathrm{char}}) = c_{\mathrm{stab}} \left(\frac{h_{\mathrm{char}}}{L}\right)^2,
    \label{eq:alpha_stab_vms_scaling}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\mathrm{stab}} \ge 0$ is a dimensionless calibration constant, $L = \max(L_x, L_y)$ is the domain reference length, and $h_{\mathrm{char}} = h_{\mathrm{nom}}$ on regular grids ($h_{\mathrm{char}} = \overline{h}$ on perturbed clouds). As shown in Section~\ref{subsec:stage5_cavity}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_stab_vms_scaling} provides an effective asymptotic calibration that diminishes artificial strain stiffening at rate $\mathcal{O}(h_{\mathrm{char}}^2)$ under refinement.

\subsection{Analysis and Treatment of Boundary Conditions}
\label{subsec:boundary_treatment}

For any node $\mathbf{x}_I$ whose subdomain $\Omega_s^I$ intersects $\partial\Omega = \Gamma_D \cup \Gamma_N \cup \Gamma_R$, the boundary segment $\Gamma_s^I = \Omega_s^I \cap \partial\Omega$ enters the right-hand side of the local weak form \eqref{eq:local_weak_form} via $I_{\Gamma_s^I} = \int_{\Gamma_s^I} \vec{W}_I \cdot \vec{\tau}(\vec{E}) \, ds$, where the complete natural traction vector derived from \eqref{eq:helmholtz_pde} decomposes into tangential curl and normal divergence components:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{\tau}(\vec{E}) \equiv \vec{\tau}_t(\vec{E}) + \tau_n(\vec{E})\vec{n} = -\frac{1}{\mu_r} \left( \vec{n} \times (\nabla \times \vec{E}) \right) + \frac{s_{\mathrm{div}}}{\mu_r} (\nabla \cdot \vec{E}) \vec{n}.
    \label{eq:traction_vector}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Inhomogeneous and Homogeneous Dirichlet Boundary Conditions}
\label{subsubsec:dirichlet_bc}

On $\Gamma_D$, where $\vec{E} \cdot \vec{t} = g_D(\mathbf{x})$ is prescribed, aligning boundary directors parallel to the wall ($\vec{t}_k \parallel \Gamma_D$) and invoking the directional Kronecker-delta property \eqref{eq:kronecker_projection} enforces $e_k = \vec{E}(\mathbf{x}_k) \cdot \vec{t}_k = g_D(\mathbf{x}_k)$ directly by nodal collocation. Denoting the assembled global system matrix by $\mathbf{A} \equiv \mathbf{K} - k_0^2 \mathbf{M} + \mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{Robin}}$ (where $\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{Robin}}$ gathers the boundary damping contributions \eqref{eq:robin_matrix} when $\Gamma_R \ne \emptyset$) and partitioning global DoFs into active interior ($\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{int}}$) and prescribed boundary ($\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{bnd}}$) unknowns yields the condensed symmetric system $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{F}$ in reduced form matching \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{int}, \mathrm{int}} \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{int}} = \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{int}} - \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{int}, \mathrm{bnd}} \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{bnd}},
    \label{eq:dirichlet_condensed_system}
\end{equation}
which reduces on homogeneous PEC walls ($\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{bnd}} = \mathbf{0}$) to $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{red}} = \lambda \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{red}} \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{red}}$ without boundary integrals along $\Gamma_D$.

\subsubsection{Natural Boundary Conditions (Neumann and Robin) and Solenoidality Assumption}
\label{subsubsec:neumann_divergence_treatment}

On natural boundaries $\Gamma_N \cup \Gamma_R$, physical electromagnetic conditions prescribe or relate the tangential magnetic traction $\vec{\tau}_t(\vec{E}) = -\mu_r^{-1}(\vec{n} \times (\nabla \times \vec{E}))$ (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:neumann_bvp_pde}--\eqref{eq:robin_bvp_pde}), whereas the regularized traction \eqref{eq:traction_vector} also contains the normal divergence flux $\tau_n(\vec{E}) = s_{\mathrm{div}}\mu_r^{-1}(\nabla \cdot \vec{E})$.
In the present formulation, natural boundary conditions are formulated under the explicit assumption that the exact solution is \textbf{solenoidal} ($\nabla \cdot \vec{E} \equiv 0$ in $\overline{\Omega}$), which holds in source-free homogeneous media and for the manufactured and cavity problems investigated herein. Under this solenoidal condition, $\tau_n(\vec{E}) \equiv 0$ on $\Gamma_N \cup \Gamma_R$, so substituting $\vec{\tau}_t(\vec{E}) = \vec{g}_N$ on $\Gamma_N$ and $\vec{\tau}_t(\vec{E}) = \vec{g}_R - \gamma\vec{E}_t$ on $\Gamma_R$ into the boundary integral $I_{\Gamma_s^I}$ (transferring $-\gamma\vec{E}_t$ to the left-hand operator and keeping $\vec{g}_N, \vec{g}_R$ on the right-hand load) yields the consistent local boundary contributions:
\begin{align}
    \text{Inhomogeneous Neumann ($\Gamma_N$):} \quad & F_a^{\mathrm{Neumann}, I} = \int_{\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_N} \vec{g}_N(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) \, ds, \label{eq:neumann_load_integral} \\
    \text{Robin / Impedance ($\Gamma_R$):} \quad     & C_{ab}^{\mathrm{Robin}, I} = \gamma \int_{\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_R} \left( \vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \vec{t} \right) \left( \vec{N}_b^I(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \vec{t} \right) \, ds, \quad F_a^{\mathrm{Robin}, I} = \int_{\Omega_s^I \cap \Gamma_R} \vec{g}_R(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \vec{N}_a^I(\mathbf{x}) \, ds. \label{eq:robin_matrix}
\end{align}
For general problems where the exact field is non-solenoidal ($\nabla \cdot \vec{E} \ne 0$, e.g., due to free space charges, non-solenoidal current sources, or material inhomogeneities), the normal regularization flux $\int_{\Gamma_s^I} s_{\mathrm{div}}\mu_r^{-1}(\nabla \cdot \vec{E})(\vec{N}_a^I \cdot \vec{n})\,ds$ must be explicitly included in the boundary formulation.
Because $\vec{N}_a^I$ are linear along straight segments $\Gamma_s^I$, 2-point Gauss--Legendre quadrature evaluates $C_{ab}^{\mathrm{Robin}, I}$ (quadratic integrand) and $F_a^{\mathrm{Neumann}, I}$ for affine boundary data $\vec{g}_N$ to machine precision (Table~\ref{tab:stage1_element_verification}), and provides fourth-order accuracy for general smooth profiles $\vec{g}_N$.

\subsection{Multiplicity-Weighted Broken Norms and Field Reconstruction}
\label{subsec:conformity_discussion}

Because $\mathcal{V}_h \not\subset H(\mathbf{curl}, \Omega)$, discretization errors are evaluated over the active subdomains $\{D_I\}_{I=1}^N$ using the \textbf{multiplicity-weighted broken Sobolev norm}:
\begin{equation}
    \|\vec{w}_h\|_{h, \mathbf{curl}}^2 \equiv \frac{1}{\bar{M}} \sum_{I=1}^N \|\vec{w}_{I,h}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}(\mathbf{curl}, D_I)}^2 = \frac{1}{\bar{M}} \sum_{I=1}^N \iint_{D_I} \left[ |\vec{w}_{I,h}|^2 + |\nabla \times \vec{w}_{I,h}|^2 \right] d\Omega,
    \label{eq:rigorous_broken_norm}
\end{equation}
where dividing by the mean overlap multiplicity $\bar{M} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|}\iint_\Omega M(\mathbf{x})\,d\Omega \approx \pi \alpha_s^2$ normalizes the global area scale while retaining the spatially varying multiplicity weight $M(\mathbf{x})$ across $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}$. For post-processing and visualization, a single field on the covered region $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}$ (where $M(\mathbf{x}) \ge 1$) is obtained via multiplicity-weighted averaging:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{E}_{\mathrm{global}}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{M(\mathbf{x})} \sum_{I=1, \mathbf{x} \in D_I}^N \vec{E}_{I,h}(\mathbf{x}), \qquad \forall \mathbf{x} \in \Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}, \quad M(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{I=1}^N \chi_{D_I}(\mathbf{x}).
    \label{eq:global_field_reconstruction}
\end{equation}

\section{Numerical Validation and Results}
\label{sec:results}

To establish the mathematical consistency, asymptotic convergence, physical fidelity, and computational efficiency of the VNMM-MLPG, we construct a comprehensive six-stage numerical verification suite on the canonical square domain $\Omega = [0, \pi]^2\text{ m}^2$ (matching the benchmark suite of cell-based VNMM-Gauss \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026} and exact analytical solutions):
(1) element-level verification of closed-form stiffness and mass matrices against high-order numerical quadrature;
(2) Galerkin patch test verification across all seven canonical affine vector states;
(3) asymptotic refinement convergence under the Method of Manufactured Solutions (MMS) for pure Dirichlet and mixed Dirichlet--Neumann boundary conditions;
(4) domain coverage and stencil containment parametric sensitivity sweeps for the integration radius scaling $\alpha_s$;
(5) physical spectral validation on a 2D PEC resonant cavity with superlinear divergence penalty scaling and orthogonal energy stabilization; and
(6) end-to-end computational benchmarking of matrix assembly time, operator sparsity, and eigensolver efficiency against cell-based VNMM-Gauss across regular and disordered point clouds.

\subsubsection{Full Circular Disks (Interior Subdomains)}
For an uncut circular disk $\Omega_s^I$ ($r_s = 0.35\text{ m}$, $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 4.0$), polar Gauss--Legendre quadrature was executed with $n_r = 6$ radial points and $n_\theta = 16$ circumferential points. Measuring the entrywise maximum matrix norm ($\|\mathbf{X}\|_{\max} \equiv \max_{a,b} |X_{ab}|$), Table~\ref{tab:stage1_element_verification} shows that the relative difference between the closed-form analytical formulas \eqref{eq:k_curl_analytic}--\eqref{eq:m_local_analytic} and numerical quadrature satisfies:

\subsubsection{Truncated Subdomains Near Boundaries}
For the truncated boundary subdomains formulated in Section~\ref{subsubsec:truncated_subdomains_analytic} and \ref{app:truncated_geometry} (flat walls, corner vertices, and interior chord cuts), Table~\ref{tab:stage1_element_verification}(a) summarizes the numerical verification against high-order quadrature.

For the mass matrix $\mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{trunc}}$, Table~\ref{tab:stage1_element_verification}(a) demonstrates the necessity of the moment-based expansion \eqref{eq:m_truncated_moments}. Simplified area scaling $\eta_I \mathbf{M}_I^{\mathrm{full}}$ incurs large errors ($156\%$ on half-disks, $42\%$ on quarter-disks, and $15.5\%$ on chord cuts). In contrast, the full moment expansion matches numerical quadrature to machine precision ($\Delta \mathbf{M} \le 2.93 \times 10^{-15}$ on flat walls and $\Delta \mathbf{M} \le 3.70 \times 10^{-16}$ on corners).

For interior nodes with near-wall chord cuts ($d = 0.5 r_s$), a small discrepancy on the order of $10^{-7}$ is observed for both operators ($\Delta \mathbf{K} = 2.09 \times 10^{-7}$ and $\Delta \mathbf{M} = 1.69 \times 10^{-7}$). The exact closed-form cap area and moment formulas derived in \ref{app:truncated_geometry} completely bypass this numerical quadrature error.

\subsubsection{Boundary Condition Integrals (Neumann and Robin)}
Finally, we verify the natural boundary line integrals along straight boundary segments $\Gamma_s^I = \Omega_s^I \cap \partial\Omega$ of length $2\ell_{\Gamma}$ ($\ell_{\Gamma} = r_s$):
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
    \item \textit{Inhomogeneous Neumann load vector} $F_a^{\mathrm{Neumann}, I} = \int_{\Gamma_s^I} \vec{g}_N(s) \cdot \vec{N}_a^I(s) \, ds$ under an affine surface current $\vec{g}_N(s)$.
    \item \textit{Robin boundary damping matrix} $C_{ab}^{\mathrm{Robin}, I} = \gamma \int_{\Gamma_s^I} (\vec{N}_a^I(s) \cdot \vec{t})(\vec{N}_b^I(s) \cdot \vec{t}) \, ds$.
\end{enumerate}
Because the shape functions $\vec{N}_a^I$ are linear in $s$, the Robin integrand is quadratic (degree 2), while the Neumann integrand with linear $\vec{g}_N$ is also of degree at most 2. Standard 1D Gauss--Legendre quadrature with $n$ points integrates polynomials up to degree $2n - 1$ exactly. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:stage1_element_verification}, a 1-point Gauss rule ($2n-1 = 1$) exhibits a truncation error of $10.1\%$ on $F^{\mathrm{Neumann}}$ and $17.6\%$ on $C^{\mathrm{Robin}}$. Conversely, a 2-point Gauss rule ($2n-1 = 3 \ge 2$) matches the closed-form analytical integrals to machine precision ($\Delta F^{\mathrm{Neumann}} \le 1.92 \times 10^{-16}$ and $\Delta C^{\mathrm{Robin}} \le 1.09 \times 10^{-16}$). Both analytical line formulas and 2-point Gauss quadrature are thus exact to floating-point precision for $C^{\mathrm{Robin}}$ and for $F^{\mathrm{Neumann}}$ under affine boundary data $\vec{g}_N$ (while providing fourth-order accuracy for general smooth boundary profiles), ensuring cell-free boundary imposition.

\subsection{Stage 2: Galerkin Patch Test for Linear Consistency}
\label{subsec:stage2_patch_test}

The Galerkin patch test was evaluated across the seven canonical affine vector states:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Rigid Translation $X$: $\vec{E}^* = [1, 0]^T$ ($\nabla \times \vec{E}^* = 0, \nabla \cdot \vec{E}^* = 0$).
    \item Rigid Translation $Y$: $\vec{E}^* = [0, 1]^T$ ($\nabla \times \vec{E}^* = 0, \nabla \cdot \vec{E}^* = 0$).
    \item Normal Dilatation (Harmonic): $\vec{E}^* = [x, -y]^T$ ($\nabla \times \vec{E}^* = 0, \nabla \cdot \vec{E}^* = 0$).
    \item Pure Shear (Harmonic): $\vec{E}^* = [y, x]^T$ ($\nabla \times \vec{E}^* = 0, \nabla \cdot \vec{E}^* = 0$).
    \item Rigid-Body Rotation (Pure Solenoidal Curl): $\vec{E}^* = [-y, x]^T$ ($(\nabla \times \vec{E}^*)_z = 2, \nabla \cdot \vec{E}^* = 0$).
    \item Isotropic Volumetric Expansion (Pure Irrotational Divergence): $\vec{E}^* = [x, y]^T$ ($(\nabla \times \vec{E}^*)_z = 0, \nabla \cdot \vec{E}^* = 2$).
    \item General Affine Field: $\vec{E}^* = [1.5 + 0.8x - 0.3y, -0.7 + 0.4x + 1.2y]^T$ ($(\nabla \times \vec{E}^*)_z = 0.7, \nabla \cdot \vec{E}^* = 2.0$).
\end{enumerate}

In each mode, the boundary-value problem is formulated on $\Omega$ in the static / zero-frequency limit ($k_0 = 0$) of \eqref{eq:helmholtz_pde} with exact essential Dirichlet conditions prescribed along the entire physical boundary: $\vec{E} \cdot \vec{t} = \vec{E}^* \cdot \vec{t}$ on $\partial\Omega$. These states span the complete 6-dimensional linear basis $\mathcal{P}^1$ (two uniform translations, normal dilatation, pure shear, pure rotational curl, isotropic volumetric expansion, and general affine field), representing canonical electromagnetic configurations (uniform fields, electrostatic quadrupoles, Faraday induction, and space-charge distributions). Because the curl and divergence of any affine field are spatial constants and $k_0 = 0$, the continuous volumetric source in \eqref{eq:helmholtz_pde} vanishes identically ($\vec{J}_s = \nabla \times (\mu_r^{-1}\nabla\times\vec{E}^*) - s_{\mathrm{div}}\mu_r^{-1}\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec{E}^*) \equiv \mathbf{0}$), while the local weak boundary fluxes along $\partial D_I$ produce the discrete local load vectors $\mathbf{f}_I^{\mathrm{local}}$ assembled into $\mathbf{F}$.

We compared the classical unsymmetric row-by-row MLPG assembly \eqref{eq:k_row_analytic} against the symmetric Subdomain Galerkin assembly \eqref{eq:global_assembly} on an $11 \times 11$ discretization ($N = 121$ nodes, 81 free interior DoFs). Two nodal configurations were tested: (a) a uniform Cartesian grid with alternating interior directors ($45^\circ / 135^\circ$), and (b) an irregular cloud with $25\%$ random nodal position jitter and isotropic random directors. Exact Dirichlet values $e_k = \vec{E}^*(\mathbf{x}_k) \cdot \vec{t}_k$ were imposed on all boundary nodes, and the partitioned linear system $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{int}, \mathrm{int}} \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{int}} = \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{int}} - \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{int}, \mathrm{bnd}} \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{bnd}}$ was solved for the interior DoFs via sparse factorization.

\begin{remark}[Patch Test Consistency under Orthogonal Energy Stabilization]
\label{rem:patch_test_stab}
    Activating the local orthogonal energy stabilization $\mathbf{K}_{I,\mathrm{stab}}^{\mathrm{local}}$ \eqref{eq:k_stab_analytic} with parameter $\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}} \in [0.1, 5.0]$ preserves consistency in two distinct ways. First, without any modification to the right-hand load vector, $\mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{stab}}^{\mathrm{local}}$ annihilates all uniform translation states (Modes~1 and 2, $\vec{E}^* = [a_1, a_2]^T$), pure rigid-body rotation states ($\vec{E}^* = [-y, x]^T$), and pure isotropic dilation states ($\vec{E}^* = [x, y]^T$) identically ($\mathbf{K}_{I, \mathrm{stab}}^{\mathrm{local}} \mathbf{L}_I \mathbf{e}^* \equiv \mathbf{0}$), keeping their relative errors below $7.0 \times 10^{-14}$ across all meshes. Second, for affine modes possessing non-zero deviatoric or symmetric shear strain (Modes~3, 4, and 7), because $\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{E}^*)$ is spatially constant ($\nabla \cdot \operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{E}^*) \equiv \mathbf{0}$), integration by parts gives $a_{D_I}^{\mathrm{stab}}(\vec{W}_I, \vec{E}^*) = \oint_{\partial D_I} \vec{W}_I \cdot \vec{\tau}_{\mathrm{stab}}(\vec{E}^*) \, ds$, with explicit stabilization boundary flux $\vec{\tau}_{\mathrm{stab}}(\vec{E}^*) = (2\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}}/\mu_r)\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{E}^*)\,\vec{n}$. Including this consistent boundary flux in $\ell_{D_I}$ reproduces all seven patch test states to machine precision ($\le 1.3 \times 10^{-13}$); whereas when applied without auxiliary strain boundary fluxes under the scaling law $\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}}(h_{\mathrm{char}}) = c_{\mathrm{stab}}(h_{\mathrm{char}}/L)^2$, the stabilization perturbation decays quadratically as $\mathcal{O}(h_{\mathrm{char}}^2)$ under refinement.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Stage 3: Asymptotic Refinement Convergence via MMS (Dirichlet and Neumann)}
\label{subsec:stage3_mms}

To assess asymptotic convergence rates under spatial refinement ($h_{\mathrm{nom}} \to 0$), the Method of Manufactured Solutions (MMS) was solved on a square domain $\Omega = [0, \pi]^2\text{ m}^2$ (with side length $L = \pi\text{ m}$) with exact analytical vector field:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{E}^*(x, y) = \begin{bmatrix} \sin(x) \cos(y) \\ -\cos(x) \sin(y) \end{bmatrix}, \qquad (\nabla \times \vec{E}^*)_z = 2\sin(x)\sin(y), \quad \nabla \cdot \vec{E}^* = 0.
\end{equation}
To isolate spatial discretization errors from internal cavity resonances and ensure a strictly coercive, positive-definite continuous resolvent operator, we consider the positive-definite shifted curl-curl boundary-value problem $\nabla \times (\mu_r^{-1} \nabla \times \vec{E}) - s_{\mathrm{div}} \mu_r^{-1} \nabla(\nabla \cdot \vec{E}) + \kappa \epsilon_r \vec{E} = \vec{J}_s$ (corresponding to replacing $-k_0^2 \mapsto +\kappa$ in \eqref{eq:helmholtz_pde}) with divergence factor $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 6.0$, positive shift parameter $\kappa = +1.0\text{ m}^{-2}$ ($\mu_r = \epsilon_r = 1$), and volumetric current source $\vec{J}_s = (2 + \kappa) \vec{E}^*$. In accordance with the Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin formulation \eqref{eq:volume_energy_form}, the MMS load vector is constructed using the exact local weak identity \eqref{eq:local_green_flux_balance}, $f_{I, a}^{\mathrm{local}} = \ell_{D_I}(\vec{N}_a^I) = a_{D_I}(\vec{N}_a^I, \vec{E}^*) + \kappa m_{D_I}(\vec{N}_a^I, \vec{E}^*)$, including the analytical flux contributions along the artificial circular subdomain boundaries $L_{sI}$. This procedure provides a controlled assessment of local approximation errors and refinement behavior under exact local equilibrium. However, because these artificial-boundary fluxes depend on the prescribed exact solution, the resulting benchmark does not independently validate the global source-driven formulation using only volumetric source terms and physical boundary data. The latter consistency question remains open and is therefore not claimed as established by the present MMS results. Refinement was evaluated across the standard sequence of six uniform point clouds matching the benchmark in \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026} with $N \in \{15 \times 15, 22 \times 22, 29 \times 29, 36 \times 36, 43 \times 43, 49 \times 49\}$, corresponding to nominal grid spacings $h_{\mathrm{nom}} = \pi/(N_x-1) \in [0.2244, 0.0654]\text{ m}$. In all simulations, isotropic random vector directors ($\text{seed}=42$) were employed to ensure that spatial convergence is strictly unbiased by Cartesian lattice alignments.

\paragraph{Explicit Definition of Error Metrics in Multiplicity-Weighted Broken Sobolev Norms:}
In accordance with Section~\ref{subsec:conformity_discussion}, the numerical errors reported throughout this section are evaluated in the \textbf{multiplicity-weighted relative broken Sobolev norms} over the active subdomains $\{D_I\}_{I=1}^N$:
\begin{align}
    \text{Relative Broken Field Error: } \quad & \varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{L}^2, h} = \left( \frac{\sum_{I=1}^N \iint_{D_I} |\vec{E}_{I,h} - \vec{E}^*|^2 \, d\Omega}{\sum_{I=1}^N \iint_{D_I} |\vec{E}^*|^2 \, d\Omega} \right)^{1/2}, \label{eq:mms_broken_L2} \\
    \text{Relative Broken Rotational Error: } \quad & \varepsilon_{\mathbf{curl}, h} = \left( \frac{\sum_{I=1}^N \iint_{D_I} |(\nabla \times \vec{E}_{I,h})_z - (\nabla \times \vec{E}^*)_z|^2 \, d\Omega}{\sum_{I=1}^N \iint_{D_I} |(\nabla \times \vec{E}^*)_z|^2 \, d\Omega} \right)^{1/2}, \label{eq:mms_broken_curl}
\end{align}
where the volume integrals on each disk $\Omega_s^I$ are evaluated numerically via polar Gauss quadrature ($n_r=4, n_\theta=12$) to sample the trigonometric modes of $\vec{E}^*$ accurately, and summing over subdomains weights each point $\mathbf{x} \in \Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}$ by its overlap multiplicity $M(\mathbf{x})$.

To systematically test the boundary formulation developed in Section~\ref{subsec:boundary_treatment}, two distinct boundary-value problem configurations were investigated:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Case A (Pure Inhomogeneous Dirichlet):} On all four boundary walls $\partial\Omega$, the tangential trace is prescribed by the exact manufactured field $e_k = \vec{E}^*(\mathbf{x}_k) \cdot \vec{t}_k$, and boundary nodes are eliminated via condensed system \eqref{eq:dirichlet_condensed_system}.
    \item \textbf{Case B (Mixed Dirichlet--Neumann / PMC):} Inhomogeneous Dirichlet conditions are imposed on the vertical walls $x \in \{0, \pi\}$, while the horizontal walls $y \in \{0, \pi\}$ are treated as homogeneous natural Neumann (PMC) boundaries. Because $(\nabla \times \vec{E}^*)_z = 2\sin(x)\sin(y)$ vanishes identically along $y=0$ and $y=\pi$ and $\nabla \cdot \vec{E}^* \equiv 0$, the exact electromagnetic boundary flux satisfies $\vec{\tau}(\vec{E}^*) \equiv \mathbf{0}$. Therefore, the boundary nodes along $y \in \{0, \pi\}$ are retained as active, unconstrained degrees of freedom and determined directly by the global system.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 4: Subdomain Scaling Bounds and Parametric Sensitivity of $\alpha_s$}
\label{subsec:stage4_alpha_s}

The local integration subdomain radius is defined in \eqref{eq:subdomain_disk} as $r_s^I = \alpha_s h_I$, where $h_I \equiv \min_{J \ne I} \|\mathbf{x}_J - \mathbf{x}_I\|_2$ is the Euclidean distance to the nearest neighbor. In the classical scalar MLPG and LPIM literature \cite{atluri2002meshless, liu2009mesh}, $\alpha_s$ is typically selected in the interval $[0.75, 0.85]$ (commonly $\alpha_s = 0.80$) to balance domain coverage against subdomain overlap. To verify how this scaling behaves for 6-node directional vector stencils ($\mathcal{P}^1$) across both regular and disordered point clouds, we examine the two geometric limits governing $\alpha_s$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lower Bound (Domain Integration Coverage $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}$ and Interstitial Voids \cite{atluri2002meshless, liu2009mesh}):} Spatial integration in MLPG is governed by the covered domain region $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}} = \bigcup_{I=1}^N (\Omega_s^I \cap \Omega) \subseteq \Omega$. In a regular Cartesian lattice with uniform grid spacing $h_I = h_{\mathrm{nom}}$, the maximum distance from any interior point to its closest node (the cell circumradius) is:
          \begin{equation}
              r_{\mathrm{cov}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} h_{\mathrm{nom}} \approx 0.7071 h_{\mathrm{nom}}.
              \label{eq:alpha_s_lower_bound}
          \end{equation}
          Because $r_s^I = \alpha_s h_{\mathrm{nom}}$ on regular lattices, full domain coverage ($\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}} = \Omega$, $100\%$) strictly requires $\alpha_s \ge \sqrt{2}/2 \approx 0.7071$. If $\alpha_s < \sqrt{2}/2$, unintegrated interstitial gaps emerge between adjacent circular disks. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha_s_sensitivity}(a), on a regular grid ($0\%$ jitter), $100\%$ coverage is achieved for all $\alpha_s \ge 0.75$ (providing a $13\%$ geometric buffer at $\alpha_s = 0.80$), whereas below $0.7071$ coverage drops precipitously to $95.7\%$ at $\alpha_s = 0.60$, $78.9\%$ at $\alpha_s = 0.50$, and $29.4\%$ at $\alpha_s = 0.30$.
          
          Under stochastic nodal perturbations ($25\%$ jitter), however, localized pairwise node clustering causes the nearest-neighbor distance $h_I$ to shrink for closely paired nodes, reducing their integration radii $r_s^I = \alpha_s h_I$, while neighboring regions become locally dilated. Consequently, with the baseline nearest-neighbor definition of $h_I$ and $\alpha_s = 0.80$, the subdomains form a partial cover ($\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}} \subset \Omega$) with measured coverage of \textbf{$94.4\%$}, leaving $5.6\%$ of the physical domain in unintegrated interstitial voids where $M(\mathbf{x}) = 0$.
          
          We must analyze the mathematical consequence of this partial coverage: if $5.6\%$ of the domain is not covered by any integration disk, the discrete variational formulation does not integrate the field equations over these interstitial voids. This omission acts as a non-conforming variational consistency perturbation, which can affect solution accuracy in clouds containing pronounced locally rarefied regions. 
          To restore full coverage on arbitrary unstructured or disordered point clouds, two methodological pathways are available:
          \begin{itemize}
              \item \emph{Adaptive Spacing Estimation:} Defining $h_I$ using a spatial metric less sensitive to localized pairing, such as the mean distance to the $k$ nearest neighbors ($h_I^{(k)} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k \|\mathbf{x}_{(j)} - \mathbf{x}_I\|$) or the equivalent Voronoi cell radius ($h_I^{\mathrm{Vor}} = 2\sqrt{A_I/\pi}$), dilates subdomains in rarefied zones and drives coverage toward $100\%$.
              \item \emph{Moderate Subdomain Dilation ($\alpha_s \in [0.85, 1.00]$):} As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha_s_sensitivity}(a), increasing $\alpha_s$ into the interval $[0.85, 1.00]$ raises coverage saturation to $\ge 95\%\text{--}98\%$ even with nearest-neighbor $h_I$, while still remaining safely below the stencil containment limit ($\alpha_s \le 1.15$).
          \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Upper Bound (6-Node Vector Stencil Containment):} Each node $\mathbf{x}_I$ constructs its VNMM trial vector field $\vec{E}_{I,h}$ over a 6-node directional stencil $\Omega_u^I$ whose spatial support radius is $r_u^I = \max_{k \in \Omega_u^I} \|\mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{x}_I\|$. For a regular or perturbed Cartesian grid, a 6-node stencil includes nearest neighbors at distance $h_I \approx h_{\mathrm{nom}}$ and diagonal neighbors at distance $\sqrt{2}h_{\mathrm{nom}}$, giving an average support radius $r_u \approx 1.15\text{--}1.20 h_{\mathrm{nom}}$. If $\alpha_s$ is enlarged beyond this limit ($r_s^I > r_u^I$), the integration disk $\Omega_s^I$ spills outside the convex hull of its generating stencil. In this extrapolation regime, polynomial shape functions $\vec{N}_k^I(\mathbf{x})$ grow rapidly outside their interpolating cloud, causing numerical degradation. Geometric analysis confirms that support domain violations ($r_s^I > r_u^I$) remain strictly zero for all $\alpha_s \le 1.10$ across both regular and perturbed clouds ($0\%$ and $25\%$ jitter), initiating only when $\alpha_s \approx 1.15\text{--}1.20$ and growing systematically for larger subdomains.
\end{enumerate}

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_mlpg_alpha_s_sensitivity.pdf}
    \caption{Parametric sensitivity of the subdomain radius factor $\alpha_s$ ($21\times 21$ mesh, random directors): (a) Domain integration coverage percentage $|\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}| / |\Omega|$; (b) Relative broken $L^2(\vec{E})$ error in the 2D MMS problem across regular and perturbed clouds.}
    \label{fig:alpha_s_sensitivity}
\end{figure}

To quantify these geometric bounds, Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha_s_sensitivity}(b) illustrates the boundary-value convergence under the Method of Manufactured Solutions on the shared $21 \times 21$ baseline grid with randomized vector directors across regular and perturbed clouds:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MMS Convergence Accuracy:} For the MMS benchmark ($21\times 21$ grid, random directors), the relative $L^2(\vec{E})$ error exhibits a smooth, stable response across the operational interval $\alpha_s \in [0.70, 1.20]$: at $\alpha_s = 0.80$, the error is $2.94\%$ for $0\%$ jitter and $4.26\%$ for $25\%$ jitter. Beyond $\alpha_s > 1.20$, extrapolation errors cause accuracy to degrade (reaching $4.71\%$ and $5.46\%$ at $\alpha_s = 2.00$, respectively). Notably, the apparent flattening of error below $\alpha_s < 0.70$ does not indicate enhanced physical accuracy, but rather an unphysical plateau caused by extensive unintegrated voids (where up to $70\%\text{--}85\%$ of the domain is unrepresented by any local test subdomain).
\end{itemize}

Based on this comprehensive geometric and numerical study:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\alpha_s < 0.70$ suffers from severe under-integration voids, failing to capture the global field dynamics across the physical domain.
    \item $\alpha_s > 1.20$ triggers polynomial extrapolation beyond stencil support radii, leading to systematic error amplification.
    \item $\alpha_s = 0.80$ provides a well-balanced operational baseline: on regular lattices, it ensures complete ($100\%$) domain coverage with a $13\%$ safety buffer above $\sqrt{2}/2$; while on $25\%$ perturbed clouds it achieves $94.4\%$ coverage (confining interstitial voids to $5.6\%$) while strictly avoiding trial support containment violations ($r_s^I \approx 0.72 r_u^I < r_u^I$, exactly $0\%$ extrapolation).
\end{itemize}
In addition, choosing $\alpha_s = 0.80$ mirrors the scale-invariant stencil qualification threshold $\widehat{\mathrm{Tol}} = 0.80$ established in Section~\ref{subsec:stencil_selection}. Adopting $\alpha_s = 0.80$ as the operational baseline throughout all simulations in this paper establishes parameter economy and preserves benchmarking conformity with classical MLPG/LPIM literature, keeping unintegrated interstitial voids confined to $5.6\%$ on the tested $25\%$-jitter configurations.

\begin{remark}[Geometric Invariance of $\alpha_s$ under Energy Stabilization]
    The bounding limits $\alpha_s \ge \sqrt{2}/2 \approx 0.7071$ (domain integration coverage) and $\alpha_s \le 1.15$ (support domain containment) are purely geometric invariants of the local partition and trial stencils, completely independent of algebraic operator modifications. Repeating the parametric MMS sweep of Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha_s_sensitivity}(b) with the orthogonal energy stabilization operator $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{stab}}$ activated ($\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}} \propto (h_{\mathrm{nom}}/L)^2$, Section~\ref{subsec:rank_deficiency_stabilization}) reveals an identical flat error profile across $\alpha_s \in [0.70, 1.20]$ (with variations below $0.3\%$). Because algebraic stabilization acts strictly within existing test disks and cannot integrate unrepresented physical voids, the selection $\alpha_s = 0.80$ remains a robust, canonical geometric foundation whether energy stabilization is active or inactive.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Stage 5: Physical Spectral Validation and Divergence Penalty Scaling}
\label{subsec:stage5_cavity}

Having established $\alpha_s = 0.80$ as the universal subdomain standard in Section~\ref{subsec:stage4_alpha_s}, the physical spectral fidelity of VNMM-MLPG is evaluated on the 2D PEC resonant cavity $\Omega = [0, \pi]^2\text{ m}^2$ ($L_x = L_y = \pi\text{ m}$) with $\mu_r = 1, \epsilon_r = 1$. The analytical cutoff wavenumbers for transverse electric modes $TE_{nm}$ evaluate to:
\begin{equation}
    k_{c, nm} = \sqrt{\lambda_{nm}} = \sqrt{n^2 + m^2}\text{ m}^{-1}, \qquad n, m \in \mathbb{N}_0, \quad (n, m) \ne (0, 0).
    \label{eq:analytical_cavity_kc}
\end{equation}
Global stiffness and mass operators $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}}$ and $\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{red}}$ are assembled via the symmetric Subdomain Galerkin scheme \eqref{eq:global_assembly}, guaranteeing strictly real eigenvalues ($\lambda = k_c^2 \in \mathbb{R}$). Physical solenoidal modes are distinguished from spurious gradient modes via the divergence energy ratio matching \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\mathrm{div}}(\mathbf{e}) = \frac{\mathbf{e}^T \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{div}} \mathbf{e}}{\mathbf{e}^T \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{curl}} \mathbf{e}} \times 100\%.
    \label{eq:R_div_definition}
\end{equation}
Physical solenoidal modes satisfy $R_{\mathrm{div}} \le 50\%$, whereas unregularized gradient modes exhibit $R_{\mathrm{div}} \gg 100\%$. Spectral accuracy across the first 10 physical modes is quantified by the mean relative cutoff wavenumber error $\overline{\varepsilon}_{k_c} \equiv \frac{1}{10}\sum_{m=1}^{10} |k_{c, \mathrm{num}}^{(m)} - k_{c, \mathrm{ref}}^{(m)}| / k_{c, \mathrm{ref}}^{(m)}$. In all simulations below, isotropic random vector directors ($\text{seed}=42$) and $\alpha_s = 0.80$ are employed.

\subsubsection{Parametric Analysis of $s_{\mathrm{div}}$ on the Baseline $21\times 21$ Grid}
\label{subsubsec:s_div_sweep}

We first investigate the sensitivity of the divergence regularization penalty $s_{\mathrm{div}}$ on the baseline $21 \times 21$ grid ($N = 441$ nodes, 361 active free interior DoFs, nominal spacing $h_{\mathrm{nom}} = \pi/20 \approx 0.1571\text{ m}$). In previous cell-based formulations \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}, a penalty of $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 6.0$ was adopted as standard. To systematically assess its efficacy in the cell-free MLPG setting, a parametric sweep across $s_{\mathrm{div}} \in [0.0, 50.0]$ was performed, as documented in Table~\ref{tab:s_div_sweep}.

In computational electromagnetics, non-physical spurious gradient modes originating from discrete nullspace leakage ($\nabla_h \times \nabla_h \phi_h \neq \mathbf{0}$) scale linearly with the divergence penalty ($d\lambda_{\mathrm{spur}} / ds_{\mathrm{div}} \gg 0$) and are shifted rapidly toward high frequencies. Conversely, physical cavity resonances stabilize asymptotically near their analytical cutoff values ($d\lambda / ds_{\mathrm{div}} \to 0$).

As reported in Table~\ref{tab:s_div_sweep}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item At $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 0.0$ (unregularized), the stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{curl}}$ is numerically singular; all initial 10 numerical modes are spurious gradient fields with dominant divergence energy ($\max R_{\mathrm{div}} > 10^4\%$), giving an apparent error of $83.23\%$.
    \item For $s_{\mathrm{div}} \ge 4.0$, all 10 computed states correspond strictly to physical cavity modes, with the lowest spurious mode shifted completely outside the operational band ($\lambda_{\mathrm{spur}} > 78$).
    \item Adopting the previous standard $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 6.0$ from \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026} yields a mean relative error of $8.83\%$.
    \item Increasing the penalty to $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 15.0$ substantially reduces the divergence residue of physical modes (down to $\max R_{\mathrm{div}} = 3.4\%$). Across the first 10 physical modes, it achieves a minimum mean error of $\mathbf{2.46\%}$, a maximum error below $4.95\%$, and stable conditioning ($\mathrm{cond} = 5.14 \times 10^4$). The slight non-monotonicity in ``Lowest Spur $\lambda$'' between $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 10.0$ ($\lambda = 252.60$) and $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 15.0$ ($\lambda = 237.24$) occurs far above the operational band ($\lambda \le 9$, near the 80th discrete mode). Near that Nyquist limit, ascending gradient modes hybridize with dispersive higher-order cavity modes across the fixed $R_{\mathrm{div}} = 50\%$ threshold.

\subsubsection{Spectral Stagnation under Fixed Divergence Penalty}
\label{subsubsec:stagnation_fixed_sdiv}

Having achieved high accuracy on the $21 \times 21$ mesh with $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 15.0$, we examine the error behavior under systematic grid refinement across the six benchmark resolutions from \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}: $15 \times 15, 22 \times 22, 29 \times 29, 36 \times 36, 43 \times 43,$ and $49 \times 49$.

When holding the penalty fixed at $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 15.0$ on regular nodal distributions (isotropic random directors, $\text{seed}=42$), the mean relative cutoff wavenumber error behaves as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \overline{\varepsilon}_{k_c}(s_{\mathrm{div}}=15.0): \quad & 6.46\% \; (15^2) \longrightarrow 3.84\% \; (22^2) \longrightarrow 5.27\% \; (29^2)                  \\
                                                                   & \longrightarrow 5.16\% \; (36^2) \longrightarrow 6.44\% \; (43^2) \longrightarrow 7.32\% \; (49^2).
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Notably, after an initial decrease from $15 \times 15$ to $22 \times 22$, the error does not converge toward zero under further refinement. Instead, it exhibits an artificial stagnation, plateauing between $5.1\%$ and $7.3\%$ for dense meshes ($N_x \ge 29$). A similar stagnation occurred with $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 6.0$ (which plateaued at $\approx 11\%\text{--}13\%$).

\subsubsection{Constraint-to-DoF Ratios and Asymptotic Divergence Penalty Scaling}
\label{subsubsec:constraint_ratio_scaling}

To understand the origin of this spectral stagnation, we observe that in cell-based VNMM-Gauss \cite{stable_vnmm2d_2026}, $3 \times 3$ Gauss points per cell sample distinct neighboring stencils, providing an over-constraint ratio of $\approx 9:1$ that stiffens the curl operator. In contrast, cell-free MLPG evaluates exactly one disk per node, yielding an effective constraint-to-DoF ratio of only $\approx 1.1:1$. Consequently, keeping $s_{\mathrm{div}}$ fixed while refining the cloud allows discrete gradient modes in the expanding irrotational nullspace to soften and pollute the physical spectrum.

To maintain spectral separation across the tested resolutions without inducing volumetric locking, we adopt an empirical superlinear power-law scaling $s_{\mathrm{div}} \propto h_{\mathrm{char}}^{-\beta}$ with $\beta = 1.5$. This calibration ensures that the penalty grows sufficiently fast to push spurious gradient modes out of the lower operational spectrum while keeping penalty energy perturbations on physical solenoidal modes small. Across all benchmark experiments, $s_{\mathrm{div}}$ is evaluated as a uniform global scalar:
\begin{equation}
    s_{\mathrm{div}} = s_{\mathrm{div}}^{(0)} \left( \frac{h_0}{h_{\mathrm{char}}} \right)^{1.5},
    \label{eq:s_div_scaling_global}
\end{equation}
where $h_{\mathrm{char}} \equiv h_{\mathrm{nom}} = L/(N_x - 1)$ on regular Cartesian grids, $h_{\mathrm{char}} \equiv \overline{h} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{I=1}^N h_I$ on perturbed point clouds, and $s_{\mathrm{div}}^{(0)} = 15.0$ is the baseline reference penalty calibrated at the nominal spacing $h_0 = \pi/20\text{ m}$ ($21\times 21$ grid). Although the largest discrete eigenvalues increase under this superlinear penalty scaling ($\lambda_{\max} \propto h_{\mathrm{char}}^{-3.5}$), the high-frequency penalty modes are mapped to small-magnitude eigenvalues by the Shift-and-Invert transformation $(\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} - \sigma_{\mathrm{shift}} \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{red}})^{-1}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{red}}$ (with spectral shift $\sigma_{\mathrm{shift}} < \lambda_{\min}$) \cite{saad2011numerical, lehoucq1998arpack} and are therefore naturally excluded from the target low-frequency spectrum by the Krylov subspace. The effect of this scaling on the factorization cost of the shifted systems is assessed separately through the reported computational benchmarks in Section~\ref{subsec:stage6_benchmark} (taking $\sim 0.024\text{ s}$ on the $49\times 49$ grid).

    \item \textbf{High Spectral Accuracy on Fine Meshes:} On the dense $49 \times 49$ grid, where the unstabilized formulation stalled at $2.32\%$, orthogonal stabilization reduces the mean error to $1.29\%$ ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$) and $\mathbf{1.17\%}$ ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 100$), improving upon the finest-grid accuracy of VNMM-Gauss ($1.53\%$).
    \item \textbf{Coarse-Grid Artificial Stiffness vs. Fine-Grid Asymptotic Rate (Engineering Trade-Off):} Table~\ref{tab:cavity_convergence_stabilization} reveals a clear trade-off on coarse discretizations. On the coarsest $15 \times 15$ grid ($h_{\mathrm{nom}}/L \approx 0.071$), the unstabilized formulation ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 0$) yields an initial cutoff error of $3.87\%$, whereas adding stabilization introduces artificial stiffness when $h_{\mathrm{nom}}$ is large, increasing the coarse-grid error to $6.69\%$ ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 20$), $11.14\%$ ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 40$), $17.67\%$ ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$), and $23.81\%$ ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 100$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}} \approx 0.51$). 
    Thus, the stabilization operator is \emph{not} neutral across all resolution regimes: on coarse grids, penalizing $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{shear}}^I$ and $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{hyp}}^I$ injects artificial strain energy that over-stiffens the discrete system. 
    As the point cloud is refined, the quadratic scaling $\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}} \propto (h_{\mathrm{nom}}/L)^2$ diminishes this penalty (dropping by a factor of $4$ by $29 \times 29$, where $\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}} \le 0.09$ for $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$), allowing the physical curl-div operator to dominate and reducing the $49 \times 49$ error to $1.29\%$ ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$) and $1.17\%$ ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 100$).
    Consequently, for applications restricted to very coarse point clouds ($15 \times 15$ to $22 \times 22$), a small parameter $c_{\mathrm{stab}} \in [0, 20]$ yields better coarse accuracy ($3.87\%\text{--}6.69\%$) at the cost of slower asymptotic convergence ($p \le 1.06$), whereas higher stabilization ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} \ge 70$) recovers near-quadratic empirical rates ($p \ge 2.15$) on the tested mesh sequence.
    \item \textbf{Convergence under Perturbed Clouds ($25\%$ Jitter):} As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:mlpg_cavity_stabilization}(b), under $25\%$ jitter, the unaugmented formulation ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 0$) exhibits a coarse-grid error of $12.67\%$ ($15\times 15$) and reaches $3.95\%$ on $49 \times 49$ ($p = 0.84$). Moderate stabilization ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 20$ and $40$) reduces the error on every tested mesh compared to $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 0$ (lowering the $15\times 15$ error to $4.44\%$ and $5.02\%$, and reaching $3.37\%$ and $2.80\%$ on $49\times 49$), which explains why the 6-point log-log regression slope appears flatter ($p = 0.18$ and $0.54$). Stronger stabilization ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 100$) yields $p = 2.30$ and a mean error of $\mathbf{1.45\%}$ on $49 \times 49$, while $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$ provides a balanced compromise across resolutions ($p = 1.62$, $1.97\%$ on $49 \times 49$, and $11.88\%$ on $15 \times 15$ vs.\ $18.61\%$ for $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 100$). This indicates that restoring full local stiffness rank mitigates discrete mode softening across the tested regular and disordered configurations.
\item \textbf{Impact on Individual Physical Modes vs. Mean Error:}
Because $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{stab}}$ penalizes the traceless strain tensor $\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{E})$, its effect depends on the modal kinematics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Fundamental Modes ($TE_{10}, TE_{01}$, exact $k_c = 1.0\text{ m}^{-1}$):} Without stabilization ($c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 0$), the numerical cutoff wavenumber stagnates at $k_c \approx 0.9551\text{ m}^{-1}$ ($4.49\%$ error). Activating $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$ reduces this error to $1.41\%$ on $29 \times 29$ and $\mathbf{0.48\%}$ on $49 \times 49$.
    \item \emph{Intermediate Diagonal Modes ($TE_{11}$, exact $k_c = 1.4142\text{ m}^{-1}$):} Where $\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\vec{E}) \ne \mathbf{0}$, $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$ reduces the cutoff error from $2.14\%$ ($22 \times 22$) to $0.85\%$ ($36 \times 36$) and $\mathbf{0.31\%}$ on $49 \times 49$.
    \item \emph{Higher-Order Modes ($TE_{22}$, exact $k_c = 2.8284\text{ m}^{-1}$):} Under $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 0$, $TE_{22}$ suffered severe nullspace leakage on fine grids ($R_{\mathrm{div}} > 30\%$); with $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$, its error drops to $1.79\%$ on $21\times 21$ and below $1.15\%$ on $49 \times 49$.
\end{itemize}
This mode-by-mode inspection shows that in the refinement regime ($29\times 29$ to $49\times 49$), the quadratic scaling law \eqref{eq:alpha_stab_vms_scaling} yields consistent spectral error reduction across the tested physical modes without polluting the lower operational band.
\end{enumerate}

Based on these comparative results, $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$ is adopted as a practical compromise for the computational benchmarks in Stage~6, balancing fine-grid convergence ($p = 2.15$ regular, $1.29\%$ error on $49\times 49$) against coarse-grid over-stiffening ($17.67\%$ on $15\times 15$).

The comparative results in Tables~\ref{tab:benchmark_comparison} and \ref{tab:jitter_benchmark_comparison} and Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark} demonstrate clear advantages of VNMM-MLPG over cell-based integration:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Assembly Speedup ($2.0\times\text{--}3.7\times$):} By evaluating closed-form analytical formulas \eqref{eq:k_curl_analytic}--\eqref{eq:m_local_analytic} over $N$ disk subdomains instead of looping over thousands of numerical Gauss points with point-in-cell tests and $k$-d tree searches, VNMM-MLPG accelerates global matrix assembly by a factor of $1.99\times$ to $3.69\times$ (scaling from $0.062\text{ s}$ on $15 \times 15$ to $0.346\text{ s}$ on $49 \times 49$, compared to $0.124\text{ s}$ and $1.275\text{ s}$ for Gauss integration).
    \item \textbf{$20\%\text{--}24\%$ Sparser Global Operators:} In cell-based integration, a single background cell interacts with all nodes belonging to the stencils of its 9 Gauss points, inflating the sparsity pattern of $\mathbf{K}$. In VNMM-MLPG, each node $I$ interacts strictly with the 6 nodes in its own stencil $\Omega_u^I$. Consequently, global matrices contain $20\%\text{--}24\%$ fewer non-zero entries (e.g., $35,385$ vs. $44,939$ NNZ on $49 \times 49$), reducing memory consumption and improving cache locality.

On the finest grids, the cutoff error stays below $2\%$, with a small non-monotonic variation at the last refinement step ($1.77\%$ on $43\times 43$ to $1.97\%$ on $49\times 49$). 
    
    On the coarsest perturbed point cloud ($15 \times 15$, $h_{\mathrm{nom}} \approx 0.2244\text{ m}$), stabilized VNMM-MLPG yields an error of $11.88\%$, compared to $24.67\%$ for VNMM-Gauss ($2.08\times$ lower). In cell-based methods, irregular nodal displacements distort the alignment between background Cartesian cells and shifting support stencils, causing intra-cell stencil transitions and quadrature under-integration. In contrast, VNMM-MLPG integrates the weak form in closed form over autonomous circular subdomains $\Omega_s^I$ using a single polynomial stencil $\Omega_u^I$ per disk. While cell-based Gauss attains a steeper slope ($p = 2.29$) and slightly lower error on the finest perturbed grid ($1.63\%$ vs.\ $1.97\%$ on $49\times 49$), it incurs a $2.0\times\text{--}3.7\times$ assembly time penalty and generates $20\%\text{--}24\%$ denser global operators.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Current Deficiencies and Open Challenges}
\label{subsec:deficiencies_challenges}

Despite its demonstrated numerical efficacy, several mathematical limitations, operational restrictions, and open challenges remain for VNMM-MLPG:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unproven Global Consistency for Prescribed Volumetric Sources and Uniform Coercivity in $\mathcal{V}_h$:} Because local $\mathcal{P}^1$ reconstructions are discontinuous across overlapping subdomain boundaries ($\mathcal{V}_h \not\subset H(\mathbf{curl};\Omega)$), the consistency error functional $\mathcal{R}_h(\vec{w}_h)$ in \eqref{eq:global_consistency_residual} contains boundary fluxes over non-paired internal circular arcs $L_{sI}$. The formulation satisfies exact affine reproduction and exhibits the reported refinement behavior when the local loads are constructed using the complete analytical subdomain boundary fluxes. Consistency and convergence for general source-driven problems using only prescribed volumetric sources $\vec{J}_s$ and physical boundary data (potentially via partition-of-unity weightings or inter-subdomain jump penalization), as well as proving uniform discrete coercivity $\alpha_h \ge \alpha_0 > 0$ as $h_{\mathrm{char}} \to 0$, remain fundamental open mathematical problems.
    \item \textbf{Partial Domain Coverage under Perturbed Point Clouds and Solenoidal Boundary Assumption:} Under $25\%$ jitter with fixed $\alpha_s = 0.80$ and nearest-neighbor spacing $h_I$, clustered node pairs shrink local radii $r_s^I$. This produces partial coverage ($|\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}|/|\Omega| = 94.4\%$), restricting two-sided norm equivalence to $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}}$; ensuring $\Omega_{\mathrm{cov}} = \Omega$ under strong disorder requires adaptive radii ($k$-th neighbor or Voronoi metrics). In addition, the simplified natural boundary treatment in Section~\ref{subsubsec:neumann_divergence_treatment} assumes exact solenoidality ($\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = 0$) to omit the normal flux $\tau_n(\vec{E}) = s_{\mathrm{div}}\mu_r^{-1}(\nabla \cdot \vec{E})$ on $\Gamma_N \cup \Gamma_R$. Non-solenoidal problems with sources ($\nabla \cdot \vec{E} \ne 0$) must retain $\tau_n$ explicitly.
    \item \textbf{Higher-Order Mode Dispersion, Stabilization Trade-Offs, and $\mathcal{P}^2$ Extension:} Rapidly oscillating higher-order cavity modes exhibit larger discrete divergence ratios (e.g., $\mathrm{TE}_{30}/\mathrm{TE}_{03}$ in Table~\ref{tab:cavity_eigenvalues}) due to the linear capacity of $\mathcal{P}^1$. Moreover, while the coupled calibration ($s_{\mathrm{div}} \propto h_{\mathrm{char}}^{-1.5}$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}} \propto h_{\mathrm{char}}^2$) mitigates mode softening in the tested operational band, it introduces a coarse-grid over-stiffening trade-off in $c_{\mathrm{stab}}$ and increases condition number growth on dense grids. Extending the Vector PIM basis to quadratic polynomials ($\mathcal{P}^2$, 12 nodes per stencil in 2D) is a natural route to confer higher local operator rank without auxiliary subgrid stabilization.
    \item \textbf{Curved Boundaries, Re-Entrant Corners, Material Interfaces, and 3D Extension:} The closed-form truncation formulas in Section~\ref{subsubsec:truncated_subdomains_analytic} and \ref{app:truncated_geometry} assume rectilinear walls and orthogonal convex corners. Extending analytical integration to curved CAD boundaries, singular re-entrant corners ($\vec{E} \sim r^{\pi/\theta_{\mathrm{corner}} - 1}$), multi-dielectric interfaces with normal jumps ($[E_n] \ne 0$, as treated in the Method of Finite Spheres \cite{nicomedes2021acoustic}), and 3D spherical subdomains ($V_0 = \frac{4}{3}\pi r_s^3$, $I_2 = \frac{4}{15}\pi r_s^5$ with 12-term $\mathcal{P}_1^3$ bases) constitute the primary directions for future work.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This work presented the formulation, implementation, and numerical evaluation of a cell-free Vector Nodal Meshless Method via Meshless Local Petrov--Galerkin (VNMM-MLPG) for two-dimensional computational electromagnetics. By combining the directional $\mathcal{P}^1$ Vector Nodal Meshless Method with weak-form integration over circular subdomains $\Omega_s^I$ and Local Galerkin testing (MLPG6), the method eliminates background quadrature cells and intra-cell stencil discontinuities.

The main algebraic and numerical findings are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Closed-form analytical integration over circular disks and rectilinear boundary-truncated segments eliminates numerical quadrature errors over the local subdomains, while directional Kronecker-delta collocation enforces tangential PEC boundary conditions directly.
    \item The additive Symmetric Subdomain Galerkin assembly avoids the refinement-related numerical instability observed in the tested unsymmetric row-by-row MLPG/LPIM configurations. It also preserves global matrix symmetry and positive semidefiniteness ($\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{K}^T \ge 0$, $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{M}^T > 0$, and $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{red}} > 0$ on fixed meshes under edge-connected PEC stencils) without partition-of-unity rational derivatives.
    \item Projecting the two local zero-energy strain modes (pure shear and hyperbolic) onto directional DoFs yields a VMS-inspired orthogonal energy stabilization operator ($\alpha_{\mathrm{stab}} \propto h_{\mathrm{char}}^2$). This operator introduces a finite-mesh $\mathcal{O}(h_{\mathrm{char}}^2)$ perturbation on deviatoric strain modes, creating a coarse-grid accuracy trade-off. Combined with empirical superlinear divergence penalization ($s_{\mathrm{div}} \propto h_{\mathrm{char}}^{-1.5}$), it mitigates local mode softening in the refinement regime with zero spurious modes in the tested 10-mode operational band. For $c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$, it achieves empirical convergence rates of $p = 2.15$ on regular grids (full coverage) and $p = 1.62$ on the tested $25\%$-jitter clouds ($94.4\%$ partial coverage).
    \item Both methods were compared under independently calibrated settings ($s_{\mathrm{div}} \propto h_{\mathrm{nom}}^{-1.5}, c_{\mathrm{stab}} = 70$ for VNMM-MLPG versus $s_{\mathrm{div}} = 6.0$ for cell-based VNMM-Gauss). Under these settings, the calibrated cell-free configuration achieves $2.0\times\text{--}3.7\times$ faster matrix assembly, $20\%\text{--}24\%$ sparser global matrices, and lower cutoff errors across most tested resolutions (all six regular grids and five of six perturbed grids).
\end{itemize}

Future work will focus on addressing the theoretical and geometric open questions summarized in Section~\ref{subsec:deficiencies_challenges}, including establishing a complete non-conforming consistency and coercivity theory, adaptive subdomain radii for guaranteed full coverage under strong nodal disorder, quadratic ($\mathcal{P}^2$) vector bases, multi-dielectric interfaces, and 3D spherical subdomains.

\section{Closed-Form Geometry, Active Areas, and Moments for Truncated Subdomains}
\label{app:truncated_geometry}

This appendix provides analytical closed-form expressions for the active physical area $S_0^I = \iint_{D_I} d\Omega$, area fraction $\eta_I = S_0^I / S_0^{\mathrm{full}}$, and geometric moments ($S_{1x}^I, S_{1y}^I, S_{2xx}^I, S_{2yy}^I, S_{2xy}^I$) required by local truncated matrices in Section~\ref{subsubsec:truncated_subdomains_analytic}.

\subsection{Active Area and Area Fraction Formulas}
For a chord at normal distance $d \in [0, r_s]$ from the disk center, the circular cap area is:
\begin{equation}
    A_{\mathrm{cap}}(d, r_s) = r_s^2 \left[ \arccos\left(\frac{d}{r_s}\right) - \frac{d}{r_s} \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{d}{r_s}\right)^2} \right].
    \label{eq:cap_area_formula}
\end{equation}
Canonical boundary configurations evaluate as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Flat Boundary Half-Disk (Case 3):} $\eta_B = 1/2$, $S_0^B = \frac{1}{2} \pi (r_s^B)^2$. At an orthogonal convex corner vertex, $\eta_B = 1/4$.
    \item \textbf{Boundary Node Near Corner (Case 3B):} At tangential distance $d_{\parallel} < r_s^B$ along the wall from a perpendicular corner wall:
          \begin{equation}
              \eta_B(d_{\parallel}) = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 - \frac{A_{\mathrm{cap}}(d_{\parallel}, r_s^B)}{\pi (r_s^B)^2} \right) \in [1/4, 1/2).
              \label{eq:eta_boundary_near_corner}
          \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Interior Node Crossing a Single Wall (Case 2):} At wall distance $d_I < r_s^I$:
          \begin{equation}
              \eta_{I_2} = 1 - \frac{A_{\mathrm{cap}}(d_I, r_s^{I_2})}{\pi (r_s^{I_2})^2} \in (1/2, 1).
              \label{eq:eta_circular_cap}
          \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Interior Node Near Corner (Case 2B):} Let $d_x, d_y < r_s^I$ denote the normal distances from node $\mathbf{x}_I$ to the two orthogonal corner walls. If the corner vertex lies outside the disk ($d_x^2 + d_y^2 \ge (r_s^I)^2$), then $S_0^I = \pi (r_s^I)^2 - A_{\mathrm{cap}}(d_x, r_s^I) - A_{\mathrm{cap}}(d_y, r_s^I)$. If it lies inside ($d_x^2 + d_y^2 < (r_s^I)^2$), inclusion--exclusion adds the exact corner-quadrant overlap area $S_0^I = \pi (r_s^I)^2 - A_{\mathrm{cap}}(d_x, r_s^I) - A_{\mathrm{cap}}(d_y, r_s^I) + A_{\mathrm{overlap}}^I$, where:
          \begin{equation}
              A_{\mathrm{overlap}}^I = \frac{(r_s^I)^2}{2} \left[ \frac{\pi}{2} - \arcsin\left(\frac{d_x}{r_s^I}\right) - \arcsin\left(\frac{d_y}{r_s^I}\right) \right] - \frac{d_x}{2}\sqrt{(r_s^I)^2 - d_x^2} - \frac{d_y}{2}\sqrt{(r_s^I)^2 - d_y^2} + d_x d_y.
              \label{eq:corner_overlap_formula}
          \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Geometric Moments for Truncated Mass Matrices}
Geometric moments $S_{pq}^I = \iint_{D_I} (x - x_I)^p (y - y_I)^q d\Omega$ evaluate in closed form:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Flat Half-Disk (Case 3):} With inward unit normal $\vec{n}_{\mathrm{in}} = [n_x, n_y]^T$, $S_{1x}^B = \frac{2}{3} n_x (r_s^B)^3$, $S_{1y}^B = \frac{2}{3} n_y (r_s^B)^3$, $S_{2xx}^B = S_{2yy}^B = \frac{\pi}{8} (r_s^B)^4$, and $S_{2xy}^B = 0$.
    \item \textbf{Single-Wall Cap Truncation (Case 2):} For wall distance $d_I \in (0, r_s^I)$ and half-chord $a_I = \sqrt{(r_s^I)^2 - d_I^2}$, linear moments along tangential ($\parallel$) and normal ($\perp$) axes are $S_{1\parallel}^I = 0$ and $S_{1\perp}^I = \frac{2}{3} a_I^3$. Second moments satisfy:
          \begin{equation}
              S_{2\parallel}^I = \frac{\pi}{4}(r_s^I)^4 - \left[ \frac{(r_s^I)^4}{4}\theta_0 - \frac{d_I a_I}{12}(5(r_s^I)^2 - 2d_I^2) \right], \quad S_{2\perp}^I = \frac{\pi}{4}(r_s^I)^4 - \left[ \frac{(r_s^I)^4}{4}\theta_0 + \frac{d_I a_I}{4}((r_s^I)^2 - 2d_I^2) \right],
          \end{equation}
          with $\theta_0 = \arccos(d_I / r_s^I)$ and $S_{2\parallel\perp}^I = 0$.
\end{itemize}
For non-rectilinear boundaries (curved walls or non-orthogonal corners), $S_0^I$ and moments can be evaluated via autonomous polar quadrature ($4 \times 12$ points) confined strictly to the disk $\Omega_s^I$, preserving complete freedom from background grids.
